### Five-aspect comparison

**Elizabeth:** Subject: a high-status historical insider and workers with their own
roles. Subject Motion: dressing, handling useful objects, walking, watching labor,
directing, listening and betraying pride through a reaction. Scene: period rooms
with active foreground/midground/background and tangible social access; occasional
mechanism enactments. Overlays are separate and restrained, not a caption system.
Spatial Framing: very near-lens opening, then varied close/medium-close selfies,
two-person attention changes, useful detail and room views. Camera: wide-looking
perspective, changing height/distance and physical turns; exact lens is unmeasured.
Real-time-looking motion is the intended adaptation; no automatic slow-motion grade.

**Chloe:** Subject: a modern visitor and distinct locals/companions. Subject Motion:
testing accommodation/food/clothing, asking, attempting access, responding to
failure, accepting help and returning to people or promises. Scene: connected
public/private environments and materially changed time/costume/context. Overlays:
some episode cards, small AI marks, provider slates and Titanic epilogue cards;
these are not a requirement to copy branding. Spatial Framing: arm-length host,
interaction two-shots, hand/object and environment views. Camera: physical travel,
attention-led turns/reframes and edited time/location changes. It does not have
Elizabeth's recurring extreme-face opening as its universal visual signature.
