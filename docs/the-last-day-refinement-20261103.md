## Where the pilot falls short

| Pilot evidence | Why it matters | Adopted correction |
|---|---|---|
| 0:16–0:18: narration promises a table without food while the image shows a full loaf | Opening picture contradicts the literal promise | Pair exact critical clauses with their visible meaning; label a baseline clearly or stage the promised absence |
| 0:30–1:30: two similar exposition packages precede developed physical consequences | Rules take over the opening instead of supporting it | Integrate the indispensable premise early while meaningful action develops; place detailed limits at the relevant mechanism |
| Twenty 30-second requests all retained as full 721-frame sources; twenty planned paragraphs contain 63–72 words each | Provider packaging imposes rhetorical and editorial regularity | Separate beats, utterances, shots and chunks; plan variable thought groups and select actual useful ranges |
| Resolved detector catalogue: 66 intervals, mean 9.1035s, median 9.0417s, range 5.7917–12.5s; 46 exceed 8s, none below 4s | Little short-insert contrast; duration alone does not prove boredom | Vary duration by explanatory job, preserving complete action/speech and actual ≥2s shots; no arbitrary fast-cut quota |
| 4:10–4:18: a person turns a working tap off during an infrastructure-failure explanation | Correct hand mechanics demonstrate the wrong cause | Stage the actual input failure and a downstream output change; leave the tap open and account for pressure/storage delay |
| 5:32–6:00: atmospheric images include a mushroom-shaped cap, then an Earth view | The icon does not clearly show fire-soot removal versus conditional persistence | Keep one source/process and a consistent layered diagram; distinguish detonation effects, fallout and soot |

| The C17 local diagram repair removes invented quantitative axes but remains a broad abstract flowchart | Correctness was repaired; explanatory specificity still needs improvement | Assign diagram deliverables early and use them to reveal a particular hidden physical relation |
| 9:32–10:00 returns to abundant bread; reality reset is mostly verbal | Callback can appear to erase fictional consequences | Make return to reality legible and resolve the established question; do not imply unearned recovery |

Cut figures are resolved local detector intervals, not a newly verified frame-exact

## What to learn from each reference

**Nepal: sustain a material chain.** Around 7:29–8:11, channel geometry, mixed
debris, obstruction and stored water remain the subject. Around 8:23–9:01,
work, failure, renewed downstream flow and a summary diagram extend the same
problem. A personal object around 9:50 changes scale from landscape to human
cost. Our lesson is connected state change and specific consequence, not the
reference's unverified casualty claims or apparently fabricated official text.

**Asteroid: demonstrate a revision of intuition.** Around 5:05–6:19, a turbulent
surface becomes a cavity, inward flow and rebound. Around 7:00–10:30, wave
profiles, spreading fronts and shore geometry connect a mechanism to different
outcomes. Our lesson is a controlled comparison and a process kept visible long
enough to understand, not treating a dramatic render as quantitative simulation.

**Lituya: carry an unresolved human constraint.** Opening sleepers, a dog, waking
and the approaching threat lead to a boat-chain constraint around 1:08–1:18.
The later lifejacket/anchor/engine/wave sequence around 8:14–8:54 returns to a
decision with a consequence. Our film's ambulance and supplies could support
this kind of continuity; anonymous one-off people do not provide the same payoff.
Historical personal details must be sourced, never invented for emotion.

**Without humans: use component failure to earn spectacle.** Seed, sprout, roots
and paving at roughly 5:48–6:08 form a material progression. Steel joints, rust,
rebar, concrete and tower structure around 6:46–7:34 explain later collapse.
The opening tower imagery becomes intelligible when revisited around 10:08–10:30.
The lesson is a setup explained and paid off, with changing timescales, not
precise unsourced predictions of when a building falls.

**Moon: use analogies and returns with a purpose.** Ordinary spinning/curved-surface
models early on lead to orbital explanations. Around 3:55–4:55 instruments,
hands checking measurements, a call, tide information and a harbor bring the
abstract change into a shared world. Coast, rooftop watcher and instruments
return as conditions worsen. Around 11:15–11:40 a friction analogy gives way to
Jupiter/Io; around 12:20 a stretching-material model precedes planetary debris.
Late cooling, rain, rock/sample imagery and the actual Moon reframe the catastrophe.
Some reference news framing, graphics and scientific claims are misleading or
unverified; do not import them. A familiar model must clarify the correct relation,
and its limits belong in the explanation.

## Sound and commentary findings

The owner's music complaint is adopted directly. Complete reference mixes span
about −14.4 to −16.2LUFS; the pilot is −16.15LUFS, so global loudness is not the
problem definition. Its actual separate bed is −27.01LUFS. The prepared correction
lowers that bed by9dB while retaining native narration input gain, giving about
−36.01LUFS for music. The final encoded correction measures approximately
−16.04LUFS/−1.20dBTP; picture bitstream and600.833s timeline are preserved.
This changes audio balance only, not the documented visual/story faults.

Full local timed ASR of all five references reveals a more useful delivery
correction than “speak faster”: overall recognized density is about126–134WPM,
and our sample is already133.8. Our speech has shorter gap-delimited runs and
more long breaks, with comparatively uniform thirty-second paragraph density.
The sound guide now directs connected thought groups, role-specific momentum,
meaningful pauses and a quieter reflective landing. Word timings are estimates,
not direct listening or proof of voice character. See the
[sound/narration guide](the-last-day-sound-and-narration.md) and its linked
measurements for the complete method and concrete mix settings.

## Production changes, not another review loop

The Frameo audit found useful exact-job safeguards, rolling collection and
native audio timestamp handling. Preserve those. The pilot's first acceptance
to last download took 20m43.145s; accepted-to-first-observed-completion ranged
285.871–481.147s. Those are dated observations, not a service guarantee or total
production time. A roughly 94.7s root-download-to-next-acceptance gap is a place
to prepare bindings earlier, not evidence that the necessary voice dependency
can be ignored.

The revised workflow adds typed readiness dependencies, separate editorial IDs,
required mechanism/ending priorities, immediate slot refill independent of
download, local graphic ownership and actual selected-runtime forecasting. It
does not install a daemon, global lock, distributed scheduler or automatic voice
matching. The one account owner must reconcile jobs across chats/computers.

The score must follow named story turns rather than the pilot's fixed 27%-of-runtime
ending calculation. Fresh installations resolve available authorized audio and
references; they do not depend on the pilot's private lighthouse cues, accepted
voice file, SSD mount, old expiry or capacity of ten.

This deep review was explicitly requested. Future production keeps the fast
default: prepare once, generate ready independent work, assemble during generation,
check obvious dependencies and one final local batch, and repair concrete faults.
No routine paid scoring, full-film ASR or channel reanalysis is added.

## Acceptance boundary

The original sample proves a complete export and delivery route, not artistic
parity. This revision supplies better instructions, worked staging examples and
portable discovery. It cannot establish that an unmade revised visual film feels
indistinguishable from the channel. An audio-only correction, if delivered, must
be labelled as such; it does not fix the documented picture/story failures.
Use an original voice and original treatment, not cloned identity or channel
branding. Further improvements should follow actual footage and owner feedback,
with findings saved in these canonical project files.
