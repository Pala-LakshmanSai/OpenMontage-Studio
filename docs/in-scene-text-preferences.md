# Text on screens and physical surfaces

Owner correction, 2 November 2026. Apply to every video style and future video.

When story information belongs on a phone, monitor, departure board, ticket,
menu, sign or another visible object, put the readable information on that
surface. Do not substitute a floating caption/lower third, or duplicate the same
information over the video. The first-class sample's Newark/boarding-time lower
third was explicitly rejected even though the phone already contained that text.

Plan the display content with the shot. Generate or composite it inside the
surface, respecting its perspective, movement, scale, lighting, reflections and
occlusion. Keep it legible at the delivery resolution. A local tracked screen
composite is appropriate when it preserves the shot and avoids paid regeneration.
Check the finished shot, including movement, for spill outside the bezel or
unwanted duplicate labels during the brief final review.

Do not add unrequested explanatory labels merely to clarify a scene. Explicitly
requested subtitles, titles and intentional explanatory graphics remain governed
by their brief; this rule concerns information that belongs inside the depicted
scene and does not erase those formats or authorize fabricated factual evidence.
Preserve each style's source, audio, budget and realism rules.
