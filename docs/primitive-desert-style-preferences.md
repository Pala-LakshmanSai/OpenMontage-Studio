## Story and pace

Build an extreme, title-led practical struggle with an immediate visible problem, consequential work, causally earned setbacks, difficult choices, an intelligible change of method and a concrete payoff. Follow [title-led extreme stakes](ai-video-global-realism-preferences.md): where the title supports it, stage severe survival trouble or credible near-death danger, grounded in forces, materials, geography and human limits. Carry fatigue, injury, lost resources and damaged shelter forward; make relief and practical progress meaningful. Escalation must grow from the established problem rather than random disaster stacking or magical rescue. Show the title's visual promise in approximately the first 0–5 seconds; establish the problem, stakes and attempted method within about 15–20 seconds. The audience should perceive a meaningful new state, decision, obstacle or useful detail every few seconds. These are editorial targets, not mandatory shot lengths or reasons to fabricate action.

The references largely show calm routine and cumulative food preparation. Extreme winter jeopardy is the **owner's fictional addition**, not a verified claim about those sources. The style name does not require desert scenery, Iranian identity, a location card or a copied recipe. Research setting-specific facts when relevant; do not present generated people, invented incidents or title superlatives as verified documentary evidence.

Keep people at ordinary efficient **native 1× speed**: decisive steps, believable weight transfer, practical grips, useful tool strikes and completed contacts. Avoid slow motion, floating limbs, prolonged reaching, repeated unproductive strokes and showing objects to the lens. Characters continue doing their work independently of the camera. A necessary regrip or breath is welcome; a display pause is not.

Reference coverage often lasts about 2–7 seconds, with longer occupied actions when useful. **Generated duration is not edit duration.** During Frameo unlimited production, stage several such shots within useful 30-second chunks; the earlier 8–12-second guidance describes the historical H3 route, not the Frameo default. Retain longer continuous action when its story earns it; do not impose a universal cutting quota or turn the film into a fast montage. Reach the requested length with meaningful story, not repetitions, slowed footage or still padding.

## Required concise plan

Before spending, freeze the title promise, full causal arc and canonical shot order once. Each shot has the canonical five-aspect spec, plus its incoming state, caused outgoing state and raw sound:

| Aspect | Specify |
|---|---|
| Subject | Exact approved people, costume and important working objects; identify each person clearly. |
| Subject Motion | Ordered achievable phases, contact, weight and partner interactions at natural cadence. |
| Scene | Place, local time, weather and environmental dynamics; list overlays separately, normally none. |
| Spatial Framing | Wide/medium/detail, foreground/background, screen positions, depth and changes during action. |
| Camera | Native playback speed, physical height/angle, focus, steadiness and motivated movement/path. |

Use `skills/creative/video-gen-prompting.md` for this canonical vocabulary. Do not substitute five aesthetic adjectives for the five aspects.

Keep a small **causal state graph**: initial condition → practical goal → visible effort/result → caused setback → changed method → resource gained → physical payoff. Record which action makes each edge possible. Each node names location, people/props, material state, consequence and next need. Cuts and plausible time ellipses may compress effort; they cannot replace an essential transfer, reset progress or conceal a major morph as continuity.

Keep one **environment ledger** with the location map, door hinges/opening direction, resource positions, weather/light/wind direction, snow/ground/wetness, footprints, cleared areas and displaced material. Update it from accepted output. Track action phases, loads, hands/tools and offscreen ownership alongside original cast masters. Camera movement must preserve the regions it reveals; an initial crop or face reference does not preserve an unseen room automatically.
