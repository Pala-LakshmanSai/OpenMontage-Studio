# Outdoor Boys: two additional reference studies

Evidence: [coastal camp](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sSsTR8qqDl4) (1675.74s); [rainforest hot springs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o6zb6_TTVWo) (1839.69s). Visual method: original 360p files sampled every 30s across each full runtime; first 120s sampled every 5s. Timestamps for visual observations are approximate sample positions, not measured cut boundaries or uninterrupted playback. Speech/music labels below come from available timestamped captions, not personal listening. Fine editing, complete actions, actual acoustic texture, music placement and exact screen/audio synchronicity remain unverified until separate full audio/video assessment.

## Coastal camp — sSsTR8qqDl4

- 0–~15s captions establish a four-day Alaska wilderness trip with shelter, fishing and glaciers as concrete goals; the 5s visual sheet shows a brief montage of walking, chopping and kayaking, then logo before travel. This is a promise plus activity preview, not a fully shown build.
- ~20–50s: road, harbor, boat ride and drop-off are sampled; at ~47–56s captions describe the ride leaving. ~55–75s he takes gear and walks to scout, with wide beach geography then close camera-facing reactions. ~75–120s turns attention to tracks, bones and campsite location; captions identify bear tracks at 1:53 and state the camp decision at 1:57. Shots alternate wide place/whole-body movement, near handheld reaction and point-of-view object details. 5s sampled views do not prove a particular cut rhythm.
- At ~2:00 captions report scouting for a spring without finding one and having roughly four liters: a concrete resource constraint emerges from an action and motivates later work. By ~3–6 minutes sampled views show wood, upright shelter frame, fabric and fire; the shelter is built progressively across distinct work states. Night interior and later daylight meal/fishing/boating views give elapsed-time progression; late rainy pack-out and boat pickup are followed by a spoken wrap-up near 27:28–27:53. Images alone do not identify exact moments of every setback or resolution.
- Transferable approach: state objective, establish departure and immediate physical surroundings, then let one real task reveal a problem; cover observable process and incremental changes rather than a sequence of unattached establishing shots.

## Rainforest hot springs — o6zb6_TTVWo

- 0–19s captions open on an explicit unresolved pickup failure after three days, before the picture returns to travel/arrival. The 5s samples begin with quick glimpses of forest travel, floatplane, hot springs, small boat and a logo, followed by airport, boat and small aircraft travel. Because this is a retrospective hook, the later pickup is a payoff; timestamps are caption-derived and do not independently prove exact screen timing.
- ~25–90s sampled frames show airport/aircraft/landscape and floatplane set-down; ~95–120s physical unpacking and equipment, then host facing camera near the lake. Caption at 1:29 notes he is alone; 1:42–1:56 describes and puts away equipment; 1:58 establishes the rainforest location. A viewer sees a chain of logistical acts before the hike rather than unexplained teleportation.
- Across the 30-second contact sheet, the trip moves from hiking/canoe to primitive shelter and food, wet forest and lake activities, hot-spring bathing, campfire cooking, night shelter and rainy departure. Captions at 28:50–29:08 report waiting five hours and the plane arriving; 29:28 onward shows return travel and home/family wrap-up from sampled imagery. The visual sheet brackets but does not measure each event's exact boundaries.
- Transferable approach: a concrete complication can be promised early and resolved only after sequential preparation, weather, shelter and activity; the hook should be paid off visibly, not merely narrated.
