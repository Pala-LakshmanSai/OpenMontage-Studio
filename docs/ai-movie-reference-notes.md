## Story first: emotional action causes spectacle

Write the whole-film spine before freezing opening sequences: title promise,
protagonist's immediate want, deeper need, central bond, opposition, world rules,
turning choices, climax and final emotional state. This is compact planning, not
a requirement to finish every shot or dialogue line before generation.

1. **Deliver the premise through an immediate problem.** MYTHRA begins with
   danger and birth; Lumz with a predator struggle and an occupied refuge;
   Island with pursuit. An opening should pose a concrete question and show a
   consequential attempt before extended history, branding or scenic montage.
2. **Earn attachment with costly care.** MYTHRA's feeding and trading at roughly
   04:20–09:50 make love material. Lumz's water offer and reciprocal blanket at
   01:48–04:03 establish safety through distance, relinquishment and initiative.
   Invent a different practical gesture tied to the new title and relationship.
3. **Make a solution create the next difficulty.** Growing the dragon consumes
   food and breaks concealment; Island's escape into water finds another threat;
   Lumz's desired rescue becomes danger after his allegiance changes. Preserve
   cause, choice and cost rather than appending unrelated catastrophes.
4. **Alternate knowledge and action.** A witness sees evidence, tells someone,
   and changes the opposition's plan. MYTHRA Part 2's book, writing and poison
   handoffs give suspense physical carriers. Track what each character knows;
   a revelation matters only when it changes a tactic, trust or risk.
5. **Give every lead agency.** Care, concealment, investigation, bargaining,
   sacrifice and refusal are actions. Avoid a protagonist who only screams,
   is carried and is rescued; their particular skill or decision must affect
   the outcome in a way the stronger companion cannot supply.
6. **Use earned reversals and persistent consequences.** An ally's offer may
   expose a different threat; apparent victory may leave an opponent capable
   of acting. Seed capabilities and constraints before the climax. Injuries,
   broken resources and changed trust remain changed afterward.
7. **Pay off an earlier action with new meaning.** Island's rejected grub later
   becomes suitable berries (roughly 03:25–05:15 and 09:40–11:50). Lumz repeats
   a neonatal rescue attempt with an irreversible later outcome. Reuse the
   dramatic principle, not those plots, characters or tragedies.
8. **Resolve the promised film.** MYTHRA and Lumz spend time on consequences
   after spectacle. Both studied Eterya uploads instead advertise a complete
   series before resolving their central problem. Our complete-movie requests
   need an original climax and emotional resolution; no promotional cliffhanger
   substituted for an ending. A sequel seed may follow an already paid-off arc.

Extreme original challenges and surprising turns remain welcome under the
[story engagement policy](ai-video-story-engagement-preferences.md), with believable
causes and persistent costs. Tragedy, pregnancy, monsters, cruelty, snowy villages
and romance are reference-specific choices, not compulsory genre ingredients.

## Scale the screenplay to the requested runtime

Keep the same causal spine at any length. A short film needs fewer relationships,
locations and turning choices; a longer film earns duration through distinct
attempts, relationship development, competing objectives and consequences.
Do not inflate duration with repetitive fear close-ups, walking, recap or grief.

Each sequence card contains: incoming state → immediate objective → obstacle →
playable tactic → changed situation → outgoing state. It also states what the
audience learns or feels and how that prepares the next sequence. Quiet care,
humor, discovery and recovery can advance the bond without another disaster.
Alternate tension and release; continuous maximum intensity makes both flat.

Use approximate duration allocations as an editing forecast, not a mandatory
beat percentage. Check that essential opening, turning choices, climax and
aftermath fit the requested range before filling secondary material. A longer
film should not be a short story padded to a quota. Dialogue grows from an
immediate objective and distinct speaker, with normal variable cadence and
room for reactions. Preserve native phrase-to-mouth ownership and complete
utterances; never slow speech merely to fill a 30-second request.

## Staging, camera and light carry the story

| Dramatic job | Prepare before generation | Reference basis |
|---|---|---|
| Establish danger and escape | Place threat, vulnerable person, obstacle, exit and sightlines; show the route before exploiting it | MYTHRA cave threshold; Island forest/root/water transitions |
| Change trust | Use distance, body orientation, hands, shared objects and reciprocal action; shared two-shots become possible as trust changes | Lumz water/blanket; Island food/hand-taking |
| Reveal scale | Put a familiar person/object in foreground and threat in the same readable depth; then use a wide for geography | Eterya office windows and vehicles; MYTHRA roof and village |
| Express public power | Block the speaker, group, dissenter and escape route; changes of allegiance alter the grouping | MYTHRA crowd/church; Lumz armed confrontation |
| Make a decision legible | Show a look or hesitation, decisive physical action and its result; a close-up must add understanding | Trading a possession, packing to leave, handoff of evidence |
| Deliver aftermath | Hold enough time for an action and realization; use the altered familiar location or prop | MYTHRA grave return; Lumz medical kit and burial |

Choose camera position from what the audience needs to know. Use wide geography,
medium relational action and close face/hand/prop coverage with a purpose for
each cut. Camera movement follows a body, reveals hidden information or crosses
a motivated threshold. Do not require an orbit, push-in or drone move in every
shot. An exact lens number is optional production direction, not reference fact.

Keep the light source physically consistent: entrance daylight, a practical
heater/fire, a window, a marked exterior sun direction. Warm shelter/cold exterior
is one observed contrast, not a universal palette. Carry source side, softness,
shadow direction, exposure, weather and time through one event. Establish travel
or elapsed time before a large environment/light change. Materials should respond
to action: wet fabric stays wet; impact leaves damage; dust, snow and footprints
have causes. Preserve raw material and human performance rules of the active style.

## Continuity is prepared once and carried forward

Maintain one compact film bible and sequence state ledger in the existing project
artifacts. These fields feed prompts directly; they are not another approval form:

- Cast IDs with original identity anchors, proportions, hair, distinguishing
  features, costume layers, voice character, relationships and current injuries.
- Location IDs with stable layout, landmarks, entrances, light sources, weather,
  surface conditions and which camera side establishes screen direction.
- Prop IDs with exact appearance, scale, marks, contents/remaining resources,
  condition, current holder/hand and physical location.
- Sequence entry/exit positions, knowledge, intention, emotional change and the
  specific state changes caused by the action. State can advance, never silently reset.
- Audio state: speaker, full line and normal cadence; synchronized action sound;
  location ambience and musical dramatic role. Voice references remain character-specific.

Reuse clean canonical identity/location anchors plus the relevant current-state
reference. Do not use each degraded output as the sole identity source. For a
true same-shot continuation, use the exact outgoing editpoint frame where the
provider supports it and compatible camera/light/motion. For a motivated cut,
use the appropriate angle reference with the same world state. Do not freeze all
independent work while waiting for a continuation: only its actual dependents wait.

An immutable sequence brief makes parallel generation coherent. Story/continuity
owner releases ready sequences; writers prepare later independent material;
one account dispatcher admits jobs; one assembly owner selects actual boundaries.
Agents share versioned briefs and receipts rather than silently changing each
other's cast, geography, voice or ending. Changed premises require an explicit
downstream state correction before more affected work is admitted.

## Thirty-second generations contain deliberate shots

Use [the multi-shot prompting guide](ai-movie-multishot-prompting.md) and its original
worked prompt. Prefer useful 30-second sequences with a small number of clearly
timed shots. A typical 4–6-shot passage can combine geography, a tactic, an object
detail, reaction and changed result, while preserving ordinary shots of at least
two seconds. Fewer held shots suit dialogue; different cuts suit readable action.
These are options, not a repeated six-shot template for every passage.

Specify cast/reference roles, incoming state, exact visible geography, timed
subject actions, framing/camera, motivated light, native speech/action sound,
explicit cut instructions and outgoing state. Avoid dense simultaneous actions,
contradictory hands/props, unmotivated time jumps or a demanded camera move that
cannot fit the shot. Keep dialogue on its visible speaker and allow breathing
room. Prompt timing is intent; actual returned cuts decide selections and joins.

Thirty seconds is a generation container, not a promise of thirty usable seconds
or a reason to retain padding. Preserve complete actions and native speech; trim
at actual boundaries and respect the cached local two-second-shot check. Use a
shorter request when the story, natural speech, dependency or model constraint
justifies it. The owner's similar waits for 5/15/30-second clips are not a measured
universal API guarantee.

## Original score and sound without repeated review

The reports suggest broad contrasts—dense danger, lighter discovery, intimate
care and restrained aftermath—but exact instrumentation and timing are unverified.
Use these as creative options. Prepare a short original cue map from the new
film's dramatic changes, not a wall-to-wall imitation of source music.

Give a central bond or idea a simple reusable musical identity; change texture,
register or density when its meaning changes. Let important dialogue, breath,
touch and consequences remain audible. A deliberate sparse passage can make the
next rise effective. Keep native physical sound and perspective across cuts;
do not layer competing scores from every generated clip beneath another track.

within the authorized film budget. No reference music, voice cloning or rights
