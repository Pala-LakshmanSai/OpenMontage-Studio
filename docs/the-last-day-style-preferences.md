## Creative contract

The subject is a process with consequences: an extraordinary premise, an
understandable physical mechanism, successive changes in scale or vulnerability,
and a satisfying answer that changes how the viewer sees the opening. Narration
connects the moving images. This is distinct from raw no-narrator AI Videos
Documentary Style, host-led Vlog Style, and supplied-voiceover real-footage edits.

Classify the title before writing:

- **Hypothetical:** establish the counterfactual assumption explicitly, keep
  subsequent reasoning internally consistent, distinguish model assumptions and
  uncertainty from established science, and return to actual reality at the end.
- **Historical/current event:** establish verified date/place, reconstruct the
  causal chain and human stakes, distinguish documented outcomes from illustrative
  reconstruction, then explain evidence, lessons and remaining risk.
- **Deep-time/natural history:** ground the environment and species, compare
  mechanisms rather than make an unsupported ranking, and end with the implication
  or corrected misconception. The top-five audiovisual study does not establish
  the dinosaur branch's exact visual treatment.

Science and documented events constrain spectacle. Follow
[story engagement](ai-video-story-engagement-preferences.md) through strong
questions, surprising mechanisms and consequential turns; do not invent a death
toll, eyewitness quotation, official briefing or present-day disaster to satisfy
the global extreme-fiction preference. Research each new factual title using
primary sources before freezing the relevant sequence. Maintain a concise claim
ledger: claim, source/date, uncertainty, illustration/simulation status, scene IDs.
Reference-channel narration is creative evidence, not a scientific source.

## Hook, escalation and ending

Apply the [pilot-derived editorial rules](the-last-day-editorial-playbook.md):
pair each critical spoken claim with the correct visible cause/result; carry
recognizable systems through changing states; make uncertainty a useful local
comparison; separate explanation beats from generation chunks. A plausible
movement that demonstrates the wrong cause fails the explanation. The opening
picture must match its promise, and the closing callback must resolve it without
silently resetting the counterfactual world.

Owner correction, 4 November 2026: keep the title-led hook short, normally about
8–15 seconds, and move into the premise or mechanism by 15 seconds. Give one
specific cause/consequence or contradiction; do not delay it with a channel
introduction, repeated promise or long topic announcement. This is the owner's
production rule, not a measured channel-wide hook duration. Essential rules can
continue afterward as developing explanation, not an extended hook.

Develop the opening picture through useful beats normally 2–6 seconds long,
preserving the actual two-second minimum. A 30-second request or a long diagram
carrier never determines the first shot's retained duration. Do not speed the
voice or insert flash cuts to make an overlong opening seem shorter.

For a typical 12–22-minute film, a useful original planning pattern is:

1. **First roughly 8–15 seconds:** one compelling concrete event, impossible-looking
   scale, revealing ordinary detail, or question, with useful 2–6-second picture
   development and no repeated narration. Preserve actual shots of at least two seconds.
2. **After the hook, within the first roughly 45–90 seconds:** begin explaining what could change,
   contradict an intuitive assumption, give a human or familiar scale comparison,
   and deliver new information toward the answer. Longer essential rules may follow
   without postponing meaningful consequences or repeating the hook.
3. **Mechanism and initial conditions:** show what must exist for this event to
   happen. Explain one technical term using a visible physical process or analogy.
4. **Escalation:** each sequence resolves one question and creates a different
   consequential question. The next threat must follow from the previous state.
   Move between object, local terrain/infrastructure, human consequences, and
   regional/planetary scale. Avoid repeated explosions with unchanged stakes.
5. **Mid-film reversal:** reveal why the apparently safe option, obvious solution,
   or familiar mental model fails. Show its cause; do not merely say it is worse.
6. **Payoff:** deliver the title's promised event and explain its result. Include
   secondary consequences, evidence or an important limiting distinction.
7. **Ending:** close the principal question, revisit the opening image or idea with
   changed meaning, and choose a topic-appropriate landing: real-world reassurance,
   prevention, survivor outcome, unresolved documented hazard or deep-time
   perspective. Any CTA is brief and follows the payoff.

Build suspense with information, not withheld essential truth. Keep an open-loop
ledger linking each promise to its later answer. A chapter is an information turn,
not a generator boundary. Do not cut away just before the explanation repeatedly.

Choose a finished target within the requested range; no fixed twenty-minute rule.
Budget runtime by information value, complete narration and useful visible action.
For shorter films compress the number of mechanisms/subplots, not speech
intelligibility; for longer films add distinct evidence/consequences, not padding.
Do not end at a sample unless the owner requested only a sample.

## Writing and voice

Use one original, stable offscreen narrator by default. Write clear concrete
sentences, contractions where natural, precise quantities only when supported,
and alternating short emphasis with fuller explanatory thoughts. Use direct
viewer address sparingly. Avoid a repeated template of rhetorical question,
ominous pause and identical answer in every paragraph.

Use approximately **120–145 spoken words per finished minute** as an initial
planning envelope, then time the selected voice. This is a production estimate,
not a measured speaking-rate guarantee. Leave space for meaningful sound and
visual comprehension. Never speed/stretch narration to repair an overlong script.

Keep accent/register/timbre consistent while changing delivery with the content:
urgent event, careful explanation, restrained human loss, awe and reflective
landing. Follow [human performance](ai-video-human-performance-preferences.md)
and [autonomous voice selection](ai-video-voice-selection-preferences.md).
Do not clone the channel's voice or impose uniform slow delivery on every line.

Frameo-first production may use supported reference-conditioned **offscreen
narration** within native audiovisual chunks. Explicitly specify offscreen
narrator; people in illustrative footage do not silently become presenters.
Freeze exact paragraph/shot-local wording and allow complete utterances plus a
clean tail. A clean accepted narration passage may serve as a permitted voice
reference for independent chunks; reference conditioning is not exact identity
proof. If a separate narration route is already authorized, generate useful
paragraph groups with one settled voice and align the visuals to actual audio.
Separate TTS is not automatically covered by the Frameo pass. Never silently
switch provider or dub an already speaking face. Preserve native visible-dialogue
pairing when brief dramatized speech is deliberately used.

## Visual grammar

Create actual moving footage, physically intelligible simulations, and selective
explanatory graphics. Avoid a slideshow/Ken Burns fallback. Alternate:

- Geography/scale establishing view: aerial, orbit, landscape or structural wide.
- Mechanism insert: material contact, crack, flow, erosion, measurement or a
  clearly illustrative tabletop analogy.
- Consequence: the same water, force, failure or time progression changes a
  recognizable place, object, infrastructure system or human situation.
- Explanatory model: accurate map, cross-section, path, comparison or timeline
  only when it answers something footage cannot show clearly.

Write all five aspects for each shot group: **Subject; Subject Motion; Scene
(overlays separately); Spatial Framing; Camera**. State initial and final physical
states, screen direction, geographic axis, weather/light and cause of the change.
Prefer purposeful tracking, slow aerial travel, steady observations of real
motion, restrained pushes and readable macro action. Do not add arbitrary shake,
crash zooms or endlessly orbiting cameras. Match camera pace to information.

Use cinematic depth, coherent light and textured physical materials; blue/gray
space, water and night can contrast with warm interiors, sunsets or heat when
the scene motivates them. Do not lock every topic to teal/orange or infer the
creator's actual lens/model/software from appearance. Maintain plausible mass,
gravity, water behavior, debris, atmosphere and scale. For mechanism diagrams,
readability and factual correspondence matter more than photorealistic spectacle.

Maintain recognizable recurring locations, coastlines, buildings, scientific
objects and cast. Narrated montage permits meaningful location/time changes;
it does not require a single traveling host or continuous selfie camera. Use
established geography or narration to make the transition legible. Follow
[global realism](ai-video-global-realism-preferences.md), preserving actual
returned state and persistent damage. Same-shot continuations use the exact
outgoing selected frame where supported; independent coverage uses stable masters.

## Editing, diagrams and text

Outline variable explanation beats before packing useful 30-second requests.
Select actual useful ranges with a keep reason; do not retain every source in
full automatically. Plan complete phrase/action boundaries and a clean ending.
Let offscreen narration bridge motivated illustrative cuts where honest; visible
dialogue and meaningful synchronized actions remain paired. Mixed native audio
does not imply isolated stems. Follow the editorial playbook's compact records,
including assigned local diagrams and story-turn sound cues.

No accidental repeated narration is acceptable, including a phrase repeated inside
one generated take or replayed across joined takes. Whole-source retention and a
completed final sentence prove neither exact wording nor absence of repetition.
Use nonoverlapping utterance contracts and actual phrase boundaries, remove the
duplicate without clipping the surviving thought, and check the final joined
opening. Follow the bounded actual-opening check in the
[editorial playbook](the-last-day-editorial-playbook.md); keep its evidence with
the edit rather than claiming a pass from planned text or waveform endpoints.

Use primarily clean hard cuts, match-on-action or motivated scale/time changes.
Let complete actions and explanations determine length. As a starting design
range, ordinary explanatory shots often need 4–9 seconds and complex mechanisms
may need 8–15 or more; these are flexible choices, not measured universal reference
statistics. All ordinary actual shots must remain **at least 2 seconds**, including
cuts inside a 30-second generated asset and meaningful screen states. Follow
[minimum shots](minimum-shot-duration-preferences.md).

Use restrained readable typography for sparse chapter/time/scale information and
purposeful explanatory graphics. This style permits those formats; it does not
request constant subtitles, floating labels over every shot, or copied news
branding. Follow [in-scene text](in-scene-text-preferences.md): information on
monitors, charts, documents or signs belongs on that surface. Build exact factual
labels/maps locally where practical instead of asking the video model to invent
technical text. Verify units and scale before release. Never fabricate a source
document or official interface to suggest evidence that does not exist.

Reference sources contain letterboxed shots and very short edits. Owner defaults
override those: **native landscape 16:9 full frame, 720p/768p; no bars, portrait
inserts or blurred sides; no 1080p without permission**. Do not crop out essential
explanation merely to fill frame; stage/generate compatible coverage.

## Sound design

Owner correction, 3 November 2026: the nuclear pilot's music was too loud.
Follow [sound and narration](the-last-day-sound-and-narration.md): much quieter
score behind the original consistent narrator, connected variable thought groups,
selective physical sound and story-turn cue placement. Start a steady underscore
around 20–24 LU below typical active narration, adjusting to actual material;
this is an owner-responsive target, not a measured reference stem ratio. The
pilot's concrete correction lowers only its added music by 9 dB. Overall-mix LUFS
alone cannot establish music/voice balance or commentary quality.

This named narrated style supports restrained instrumental underscore, contextual
ambience and selective synchronized effects; do not inherit another documentary
style's silence-only rule. Narration remains intelligible and forward. Let music
change with explanation, escalation and aftermath; use breathing space rather
than an impact sound on every cut. Avoid generic trailer drums throughout,
unrequested vocals and a new music restart every 30 seconds.

Plan sound before generation. With Frameo native audio, request appropriate
ambience/effects and intentional narration; prevent unwanted extra voices or
inconsistent score fragments. Reuse a coherent authorized music source where
available. For separately authorized generated background cues, follow
[music providers](ai-music-preferences.md): fal.ai Lyria 3 Pro for flexible
underscore, ElevenLabs Music through fal only for precise timing. The pass does
not grant an external music budget. A music-free brief takes precedence.

Preserve audio presentation timestamps and use `lib/native_audio_assembly.py`
where required before sample-based mixing. Complete utterances, sensible levels,
smooth ambience and a finished ending matter more than a loud constant bed.
Audio measurements do not establish perceived voice quality or lip sync.
