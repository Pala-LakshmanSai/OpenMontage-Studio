## Story and promise

Lead with the title's concrete challenge and an outsized but plausible experience. Build strong retention through a causal disruption, consequential and costly choice, secondary consequence, and an earned payoff. The audience must understand what can be lost and why the next decision matters. Plan a remarkable set piece appropriate to the title; show practical setup and aftermath. Extreme scale is welcome. Trouble must follow established causes and credible logistics; dramatic fictional events are allowed, with internal provenance. Avoid arbitrary coincidences and causeless catastrophe. Do not invent travel entitlements or present unsubstantiated claims as fact. Retention is a creative aim, not a guaranteed metric. Distinguish fictional or hypothetical scenarios internally and do not fabricate proof of a real trip.

Owner emphasis, 2 November 2026: very extreme creative ambition is a default at the writing stage, not an optional visual flourish. Design at least one major title-relevant predicament or extraordinary experience that ordinary travel filming would struggle to stage, then build the difficult choice, attempted solution, consequence and visible payoff around it. Escalate stakes through the story rather than merely adding louder music or sensational captions. Keep physical events and logistics intelligible and plausible within the fictional journey; tension must not depend on unexplained miracles. Plan open questions and resolve earlier ones while the central challenge grows harder.

For travel, reveal the journey through sequential decisions: arrival and constraint, access or boarding, feature in use, sensory response and candid assessment, rest or transfer, and a next objective. Vary each leg with a distinctive feature or experience, allow relevant human interactions, and reserve a visible resolution of the title, elapsed time and costs where appropriate. Do not pad with repeated logistics or unrelated activities.

## Visual and audio grammar

Owner refinement, 3 November 2026: make the footage feel real and alive rather
than like a sequence of still images. Give every shot an explicit, believable
source of visible movement: a gentle physical handheld pan, small sidestep or
tracking move; meaningful subject activity; or natural environmental motion.
A steady camera is appropriate when the action or environment already supplies
visible life. Do not force a camera move into every shot or add irrelevant action.

For a harbor wide, a slow physical move along the railing can reveal the ferry
while water ripples, the vessel subtly bobs, flags move or people go about their
work. For cabin, lounge, meal and phone details, use a small purposeful camera
turn, a real interaction or restrained ambient activity. Keep normal real-time
motion: a slowly moving camera does not mean slow-motion playback. Preserve
rigid architecture, natural parallax, readable screen text, available light,
weather and coherent location sound. Avoid dead still-image holds, decorative
digital zoom, artificial shake, warped geometry and motion that distracts from
the story. Specify the intended camera/action/environment motion before generation;
do not rely on the word "video" to produce a lively shot. Apply the existing
minimum-shot-duration and fast-production preferences alongside this direction.

Apply `docs/in-scene-text-preferences.md`: embed phone/status/boarding information
inside the depicted screen or surface. Do not duplicate it in a lower third or
add unrequested floating explanatory labels. This owner correction supersedes
the earlier sample's editorial label treatment.

## Efficient execution

This is an autonomous completion workflow. Skip unrequested paid assessments and

within the actual budget, preserving the selected cast, voice and story. The cost
preference adds no mid-production approval or score gate.

Freeze the premise, causal beats, state and shot plan once. Use parallel independent ready roots, cache verified unchanged references and conform while generation runs.
