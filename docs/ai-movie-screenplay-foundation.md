## 1. Review and adapt the whole story first

Treat a supplied story as an outline to adapt, not a literal screenplay or shot
list. Read through its actual ending and epilogue. Review the entire dramatic
construction and write a coherent revised screenplay before generating images,
video, speech or music. Provider discovery, budget checks and reading accepted
assets can proceed; those do not commit the film to an unreviewed narrative.

Preserve the story's identity: central dramatic engine, emotional core, character
motivations/arcs, curiosity and revelation order, hooks, meaningful setups/payoffs,
climactic choice, consequences and ending. Preserve explicit owner requirements.
Improve how these are expressed. Add necessary connective action, supporting
character agency, conflict, decisions, reactions or grounding. Remove repetition,
boring exposition, redundant incidents and coverage that weakens engagement.
Reorder or combine scenes when it strengthens causality without breaking those
obligations. Do not replace the premise with a different familiar plot unless the
owner requests a new story. Adaptation authority is not authority to spend more
than the actual budget or change a requested factual account.

Put this review in the existing script artifact's `metadata.narrative_foundation`:
source/end read, protected core and obligations, concrete adaptation changes and
reasons, revised whole screenplay path/hash, dramatic sequences with purposes and
performance time budgets, required causal beats and deliberate ellipses, plus any
unresolved structural problem. Resolve structural gaps before media generation.
An author-written `ready` flag alone is not a review. Record what was changed or
why a reviewed passage already works. No fabricated quality score or new approval
ceremony. Full-run authorization remains sufficient for routine creative decisions.

The revised screenplay includes all sequences, their action/interaction, relevant
speech, emotional turns and transitions through the ending. A list of clip IDs or
an ending-aware logline is insufficient. Exact lens choices, shot prompts, all
reference images and detailed provider payloads can still be prepared incrementally
**after this whole-film foundation is coherent**. Reuse settled research/voices.
Then generate independent ready sequences in parallel, refill actual free shared
capacity and assemble useful returns as production continues.

## 2. Build scenes around experienced changes

Each sequence needs an immediate objective, resistance, meaningful attempt,
change and consequence. Ask what viewers understand, feel and anticipate before
and after it. Protect intrigue by controlling who knows what and when. Plant clues
before dependent payoffs; payoffs must be seen or otherwise intelligibly experienced.
A surprise may change interpretation, but confusing identity, geography or missing
cause is not productive mystery. Vary pressure and release; quiet interaction must
change attachment, expectation, relationship or choice rather than hold a pose.

For each critical transition, specify the necessary means between states:
notice/decision, departure or approach, orientation, supported contact, operation,
result and response. Use only the phases the viewer needs. Follow secondary actors
and the group when their independent action changes the outcome. A rescuer cannot
simply appear in the water holding someone; establish arrival, feasible rescue
method and first effective contact, or a deliberate legible offscreen equivalent.
When someone leaves a celebration, make the departure and any consequential group
response readable before showing them already at the emergency.

Do not mechanically show every footstep, every journey or everyone's reaction.
An established intention followed by a clear destination, a subjective unconscious
interval or an unimportant journey can support an ellipsis. Record the cue and why
the omitted action is inferable and dramatically expendable. Mark meaningful time
jumps, flashback entry/return and parallel action with legible picture/sound context;
a metadata age change does not tell the audience that seven years passed.

Maintain relational geography and physical state across actual edit points:
identities/ages, clothing, wounds, death, missing possessions, supports, contact
surfaces, vessel/architecture/technology, scale, weather/light and active threats.
A reverse angle must remain the same place and interaction. References and actual
returned pixels must agree; textual labels cannot correct a conflicting image.

## 3. Give action and emotion enough screen time

Runtime is a flexible creative target unless the owner explicitly makes it hard.
For an approximately 30-minute brief, the owner explicitly permits roughly 45 or
55 minutes when needed for an excellent movie; a shorter strong version is also
allowed. The phrase "60% more" is not an arithmetic cap that overrides those
examples. This is permission to use necessary time, not a default expansion or a
reason to pad. Budget and pass expiry remain real constraints; prioritize/rewrite
unsubmitted material coherently rather than silently buying more or cutting causes.

Estimate scene time from performed behavior: establish relevant geography, let
speech/action finish, show the consequential response, and leave motivated
anticipation/aftermath. Dialogue word count and source chapter timestamps are not
screenplay timing. Allow a difficult decision or grief exchange more room than a
simple route change. First reallocate redundant holds before expanding total time.

A provider's 30-second container is an acquisition unit. Several requests may
serve one dramatic scene; one request may span several useful beats. Never impose
four equal shots or one request per source paragraph. The two-second actual-shot
minimum is only a floor. It does not make a rushed action or emotional exchange
readable. Do not manufacture dramatic breathing by freezing, looping, slowing
ordinary motion or stretching a quiet tail to pass a duration check.

## 4. Carry the plan into returned footage and editing

Map each required causal/emotional beat to an observed useful source range and an
actual selected cut. Record what visibly happens, not what the prompt requested.
For deliberate offscreen action/ellipsis record its observed cue and consequence.
Unknown or missing critical action remains a gap; `downloaded`, `usable: true`,
all requested jobs delivered, full dialogue words and technical decode do not
establish narrative coverage. A missing bridge must not be marked covered merely
because the before/after states exist in two clips.

Inspect actual continuation dependencies and the key action/revelation when
selecting a sequence. Reuse this local observation; no automatic per-clip ASR,
paid review service, quality scoring or repeated whole-film review. Hold only
work depending on an unresolved returned state; continue independent ready work.
Repair at the responsible layer within authority: revise unsubmitted screenplay,
choose compatible coverage, change an edit point, or make a justified targeted
pickup. Test that repair against its neighbors. Do not disguise a state jump with
a dissolve/crop or retain contradictory footage to preserve nominal runtime.

The editor assembles dramatic sequences, not a sorted list of successful files.
Keep complete native speech-picture pairing, action completion and useful listener
response. Cut because attention, information, action, emotion or a deliberate
ellipsis changes. Sound can bridge continuous space/time; it cannot explain a
missing decisive action by itself. Jump cuts are intentional exceptions justified
by the storytelling, never the default consequence of generation boundaries.

Use the existing brief final moving-output check to assess flow across sequences,
identity/world state, essential causes/payoffs and picture/native sound finish,
plus the cached technical checks. Additional review follows concrete faults or an
explicit owner request. Report what was actually watched/listened to; sampled
frames, an ASR transcript or background playback are not full audiovisual review.
Technical delivery and owner artistic acceptance are separate statuses.

## Pipeline contract and portability

Initialize every new AI Movie with `lib.checkpoint.init_project` and explicit
`style_playbook="ai-movie"`; do not handwrite an unversioned marker. These projects carry
`narrative_contract_version: 1`. Before the assets stage starts (and before any
media submission), `lib.narrative_continuity.validate_foundation` checks the saved
reviewed screenplay/hash, protected core, adaptation decisions, full sequence
coverage and absence of unresolved structural gaps. Script completion uses the
same check. Direct registered-tool dispatchers must run it before admission too.

Before edit completion or compose/publish starts, the same module checks that
required beats have recorded observations inside selected cut ranges, the coverage
matches the current screenplay and cuts, and no known critical gaps remain.
These are integrity checks on recorded evidence, **not a machine certification of
visual meaning, engagement, lip sync or error-free continuity**. An agent must
actually inspect and truthfully record the key actions. No paid scorer is added.
Legacy accepted projects are not forcibly rewritten. For new media work in a
legacy movie, adopt this contract deliberately before new submissions; do not
replay accepted/uncertain work. Owner-stopped movies stay stopped.

Repository routing, style/template, stage directors, validator and example live
in this checkout. No personal memory, previous chat, GPT Space or owner's absolute
media path is required. See `docs/examples/ai-movie-narrative-contract.json` for the
artifact shape and `tests/test_narrative_continuity.py` for failure examples.
