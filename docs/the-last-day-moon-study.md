## Visual and text observations

Hard cuts and scale changes dominate inspected samples. Blue-black space and
water contrast with warm interiors and orange catastrophic heat. There are broad
landscape/planetary vistas, shallow-focus material inserts and human silhouettes
for scale. Some passages are nearly still; others show changed water, rotating
objects, fragment motion or evolving impact states. This supports a mixed
moving-illustration approach, not a requirement to generate a static-image montage.

DAY4 appears in distressed bold white uppercase around700 seconds. Many scientific
displays are diegetic monitor inserts; constantly animated subtitles are absent
from inspected frames. The source has letterboxing. New work follows the owner's
full-frame landscape rule instead. Recurring rooftops/coasts are a useful recall
device, but literal repeated spectacle and inconsistent apparent lunar scale
should be improved through deliberate state changes.

The last90 seconds move from reality measurements to ocean/metal analogy, early
Earth/natural history, normal rooftop Moon, shore, planetary origins/rings and
the familiar observer. Samples near1250–1254 show the watcher then Earth/Moon,
not a prolonged end-card panel. The ending changes catastrophe into appreciation
of the real system and returns to a familiar visual motif.

## Durable lesson

Use a bounded impossible premise, early visible stakes, concrete physical analogy,
accelerating consequences, an answered intervention question and a late conceptual
reversal. Follow through after the spectacular climax. The reality check and
repeated ordinary viewing position are essential to the ending's meaning. Save
this research once; future films use the fast production policy, not dense per-clip
inspection or repeated paid reviews. Source hashes and sample coverage are in
[the coverage receipt](evidence/the-last-day-20261103/moon-coverage.json).
