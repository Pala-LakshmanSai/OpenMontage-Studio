## Creative promise

Follow people doing meaningful work in a particular place. Tell the story through what they need, the practical steps they take, the result and the next need. Show enough intermediate action for the audience to understand how things happen. One filming sequence can contain several shots: generation chunks are not automatic scene endings. Revisit the same people, tools and locations as their states evolve. Give small everyday actions and the relationship between people room to breathe. A short film compresses a day by selecting complete linked actions, not by turning every shot into a different activity.

The film must feel like continuously observed life through an engaging, title-led story. Continuity means coherent people, action, geography and consequences across sequences; it does not mean an unbroken take, one camera position or staying on a repetitive chore. Build connected sequences in which a practical need leads to work, a useful result and the next need. Select the useful action and its consequences, varying motivated coverage and location as the story requires. A provider's duration is a generation limit, not a shot-duration target: trim repetition and do not retain a full take merely because it was generated. Natural cuts and clear, plausible time ellipses may advance the story, but cannot hide missing essential work, a broken continuation or a world-state reset.

The owner further clarified on 2 November 2026: the person is doing the work and being filmed, not showing things to the viewer. Stage work that continues independently of the camera. Hands grip a log to carry/feed it, not rotate and hold it for inspection; people do not wait for a close-up, present objects, pose, address the lens or pause so the camera can admire a detail. The camera finds useful detail within ongoing work and follows its natural rhythm. A genuine practical pause, safe regrip or interaction with a partner can remain when it serves the task; do not invent a display hold to fill generated duration. Review this distinction in actual motion, since a prompt saying "observational" does not prevent demonstrative acting.

## Story, narration and structure

1. Identify a specific setting, people, period and practical need. Establish the situation visually and early, with a clear ordinary action rather than an abstract montage.
2. Map a title-led causal story across connected sequences: preparation, effort, a real practical constraint where present, adjustment, result and the natural next need. Interleave simultaneous work only if both people's location, elapsed time and state remain intelligible. Return to unfinished processes and pay them off. Do not spend the film on repeated strokes of one chore after the audience understands it; each retained passage must add a new state, decision, useful detail, relationship or consequence.
3. Shape pacing around the activity, not a fixed seconds-per-shot quota. Hold long enough to understand hand contact, cause and result; use close detail, medium activity and occasional establishing views only where they add understanding. Brief, motivated landscape, wildlife or environmental details can establish place or mark a pause, but cannot hide a continuity defect. Allow quiet and ordinary moments; do not manufacture danger, emotional reactions or a trailer climax.
4. Default to no narrator and no added music. Let the actual activity and its raw synchronized sound tell the story. Add narration only when the current brief explicitly requests or approves it; then keep it factual, restrained and sparse. Distinguish short dialogue, on-screen captions and narration; do not infer audible narration from caption text. Do not invent quotations from real people. If visible speech is requested, preserve verifiable native synchronized picture and voice rather than dubbing arbitrary TTS over mouths.
5. End on an earned action, result or quiet continuation of life. Avoid a synthetic moral, forced survival claim, unrelated montage, or a sudden fade that abandons a task.

For the requested 15–20-minute documentary, plan the complete chapter/story arc before long-form generation. Derive chapters from the title's actual practical needs and dependencies, with meaningful changes and payoffs rather than stretching each task to fill time. Track both people's parallel work, transitions between work areas and unfinished needs across the day. Compress that approach into the new one-minute story sample: show connected setup, development and result/next need, including representative coverage and motivated camera movement. That historical sample gate was satisfied for the ensuing film. For a new production use its current authorization and canonical checkpoint policy; do not reopen settled sample approval or infer owner acceptance of a different film.

## Staging, camera and texture

Create a place map and film bible before making shots: identity, costume, objects and their ownership, room geography, doors, work surfaces, hearth, exterior routes, light, weather, season and time. Give each person visible and offscreen positions and states. List action start, hand and object contact, intermediate change, end state, and causal bridge for every shot. Record deliberate time ellipses explicitly; absent people and props do not reset.

**Character and world consistency are essential to this style.** Lock each recurring
person's face, age, build, hair, distinctive features, voice when used, clothing,
and any changes caused by the story. Maintain identity and physical relationships
in wide, close, moving and partial views; do not substitute a similar-looking
person. Compare character masters and neighboring shot anchors together, then
inspect each complete moving take and its joins before it informs later shots.
Carry the actual accepted take's positions, gestures, clothing and contacts into
the next shot. Apply the same scrutiny to props, rooms, accumulated work, weather,
light, camera geography and continuous sound. If identity or another essential
state changes without an established cause, reject the affected take rather than
hide or normalize the inconsistency. The first-pass spending rules still govern
any paid replacement.

Keep original approved character masters as fixed identity references throughout production. For a supported same-shot continuation, use the actual reviewed outgoing frame with those original masters. A short preceding accepted motion reference is an optional helper where supported, not a mandatory input or continuity guarantee. Do not progressively replace the identity masters with increasingly drifted generated faces. A reused first frame, seed, description or reference attachment does not prove identity or seamless motion: compare faces and distinguishing features against the original masters throughout each moving take, then inspect both sides of the encoded join. A continuation must advance from the accepted final action phase, not replay its preceding movement.

Judge physical progress independently of identity and a matching first frame. A take that repeats movement while leaving the work effectively unchanged fails a planned state-changing beat, even when its characters and room match. In the 2 November rebuild, `work-02` retained the pair and starting state but repeated front-strip shovel/brush cycles without establishing the planned new middle clearing. The motion reference may have encouraged replay; that causal explanation is a hypothesis, not a proven provider behavior. For this diagnosed failure, a targeted repair can omit the optional motion reference while retaining the exact actual starting frame and original identity masters, and describe one understandable new state change. Inspect the result and joining motion before descendants. Evidence: `projects/amish-winter-documentary-rebuild-20261102/artifacts/qa/work-02-independent.md`. This does not require every film to be a fixed-camera single take or prohibit useful motion references.

For an important material change, plan and review the before and intended after states. Where supported and useful, pair the actual accepted starting frame with a reviewed ending keyframe and the original identity references. Change only the intended work state in that target; audit faces, poses, tools, framing, geography and lighting against the accepted start before generation. Do not regenerate every anchor or require ending keyframes for every take. In this rebuild, omitting the optional motion guide helped repair `work-02`, but first-frame and face references alone still left `work-03` repeating work over already exposed stone. A reviewed snow-state ending anchor on the same H3 Max route produced a repaired take with visible, persistent additional clearing in root and independent four-frames-per-second review. This is one observed repair, not a guaranteed provider behavior or proof of final-film acceptance. Evidence: `projects/amish-winter-documentary-rebuild-20261102/artifacts/qa/work-03-independent.md`, `work-03-end-anchor-independent.md` and `work-03-state-repair-independent.md`.

Matching the target endpoint is insufficient. Inspect the complete motion to establish that tool contact and visible work cause the material change, rather than melting, morphing, disappearing or jumping to the target. Preserve the actual generated outgoing state for descendants, even when it differs slightly from the intended endpoint. Accept a credible optional gesture, such as the second person naturally joining the work, when identity, contact, progress and continuity remain sound; repair essential defects under the existing spending policy rather than imposing a no-second-pass or no-extra-cost rule. Ending-anchor checks do not replace normal-speed, audio or encoded-join review.

Plan what a moving camera will reveal, not only its starting crop. A tight anchor and face references may preserve the visible subject while the generated move invents ordinary objects, architecture or extra people in newly revealed space. Include the intended revealed region in a reviewed world/start anchor where practical, and record its actual visible/offscreen inventory. For inventory-critical shots, reviewed first and intended last states can help constrain the result; explicitly preserve an empty basket or shelf when that emptiness motivates the story. In the November story sample, a prompt-only stove-loading take introduced unearned kindling/logs into an empty basket and cup-stand shelf; reviewed endpoint constraints repaired that inventory in the subsequent take. This is a targeted observed tactic, not a requirement for extra anchors on every shot or a guarantee of provider behavior.

A fixed source crop may exclude an irrelevant peripheral defect only when it retains the full essential action, cast, actual camera movement and necessary geography, with neighboring continuity and final framing reviewed. Cropping must not hide a missing essential transfer or state change, remove required evidence, or manufacture a camera move. Do not add synthetic zoom/pan to disguise the crop. Preserve the original source and document the actual selected framing and its limits.

Follow the [global AI video realism preference](ai-video-global-realism-preferences.md): within a single day, track local time and location alongside sun direction and height, shadows, light intensity and color, clouds, precipitation, wetness, wind, haze and ambience. Keep consecutive views coherent while allowing physically plausible, clear changes over minutes and hours; do not invent precise weather observations or forecasts.

Camera is an unobtrusive observer, not a vlog host. Use varied wide, medium, over-shoulder and consequential detail coverage to help the viewer follow the story. Move the camera physically when the action motivates it: follow a person carrying something, make a short pan to a relevant action/result, or track alongside a route. Plan believable camera position, path and destination, with stable geography, contact, screen direction and sound perspective. Static views are useful choices, not the default for the entire film; reject still-feeling holds and repetitive coverage that add no story value. Do not replace motivated movement with a decorative synthetic zoom, orbit or push-in. Do not create a rapid music montage or impose a hard seconds-per-shot quota. Match eyelines, hand/prop contact, lighting and action phase across cuts. Follow actual accepted state; an exact outgoing frame helps only for a continuation of that same shot and never proves seamless motion. A new view requires reviewed matching action and geography rather than blindly reusing a frame. The Vlog Style selfie-turn rule applies only if a selfie-to-environment camera device is specifically requested.

Maintain ordinary available light, honest skin texture, plausible working clothes and real-looking material wear. Reject waxy faces, beauty smoothing, HDR halos, wax or plastic materials, over-sharpening, glossy art-directed light, elastic architecture, geometry drift, zoom breathing, synthetic push-ins, artificial whip transitions, exaggerated slow motion and decorative grain used to hide failed output. Keep moving foreground and background scale, hands, anatomy, object grip and shadows physically coherent over time. No portrait footage, blurred sides, borders or black bars.

## Sound

**Raw activity sound is the default soundtrack. No added music and no narrator unless the current brief explicitly requests or approves them.** Preserve native synchronized sounds of visible work: contact, scraping, footsteps, cloth, breath, tools and materials, with believable room tone or weather. Native generated sound must still be checked against the picture; do not accept unrelated impacts, voices, a score, or repeated sound merely because it arrived with the video.

Keep one coherent acoustic perspective through the action. Preserve appropriate sound tails and the changing distance of people and tools; do not reset ambience at every generation boundary. Use additional Foley only to repair a specific missing or defective sound, timed to the actual visible contact and appropriate material, intensity and distance. Generic activity beds and unrelated effects cannot replace synchronization. Do not loop accidental speech or model-generated music as ambience, or mask a failed continuation with louder sound.

If the owner explicitly requests music for a particular film, apply `docs/ai-music-preferences.md` to that approved cue only. Its Lyria/ElevenLabs routing is a provider choice, not an instruction to add music. Probe the actual final soundtrack and inspect the encoded joins. Never describe waveform, ASR or an audio-model assessment as direct listening.
