## Transferable creative mechanics

Each title states a test; early images preview a few later conditions, then chronological travel begins. The host repeatedly identifies a specific need, performs visible physical work, shows whether it worked, and lets the result generate the next problem. Setbacks need a cost and a decision: wet ground changes bed design, limited light changes work, frozen equipment changes departure. Food and comfort are visible earned payoffs, followed by another need; they are not arbitrary filler. Sleep, morning and weather appear as transitions with persistent gear and different light. Selfie narration coexists with placed work views and object details; no numerical host-face percentage is established by these samples.

## Separate user-requested creative extension — not a claim about these references

The user requests an extreme fictional glacier crisis. One connected Day 4 scene can escalate **visible warning → ice slab falls at the established mouth, leaving one clearly visible narrow gap → failed effort to move the separate slab → choice to leave bedding → one emergency bag pushed through the same gap → host exits through that gap → exposed cold creates the next survival problem**. The gap, slab and entry landmarks must be laid out before the fall and remain spatially consistent; hairline ice alone is not proof of imminent collapse. Depict gravity and limited ice debris without a magical new opening or unsupported rapid excavation. Carry the cost into days 5–7 rather than restoring the old cave offscreen. This is a proposed dramatic construction, not an observed event in the four references or practical safety advice. Any 8–15-second AI shots are a production hypothesis until motion, sound and continuity pass review.
