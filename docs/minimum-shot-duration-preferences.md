# Minimum shot duration for every video style

Owner correction, 3 November 2026. Applies to every current and future video style,
including AI movies, travel/vlog styles, AI documentaries, real-footage documentaries,
avatar videos and mixed productions.

Do not include abrupt one-second clips, flash cutaways or brief spillover from the
next scene. Ordinary visible shots must last at least **2.0 seconds**. Give an
important action, reaction or readable phone/screen state enough time to register.
This is a minimum, not a target or permission to pad a shot. Shorter editorial shots
require an explicit owner request for that video and a recorded reason and interval.

Apply the rule to actual shots inside generated or sourced video, as well as the
editor's clip selections. A 24-second generation can contain several scenes and
unwanted short fragments. Prompted timestamps are intentions; they are not evidence
of where the returned footage cuts. Partition multi-scene outputs at their actual
scene boundaries, including meaningful phone/status changes, before interleaving
them with other footage. Do not leave a glimpse of the next location or screen state
before cutting away and returning to it later.

Keep complete speech with its synchronized picture, coherent ambience, useful
action and causal story order. Correct diagnosed fragments with actual source-bound
edits or appropriate continuous coverage. Do not stretch speech, freeze a frame,
repeat footage, remove required words or spend on a reroll merely to satisfy the
minimum. Preserve every accepted source and previous export.

Use one lightweight, local actual-output cut scan and the planned edit durations.
The shared checkpoint guard must reject unresolved detected shots below the minimum
before a new compose/publish completion. Cache unchanged file results; do not add
paid assessments or repeated reviews. A detector candidate may instead be a flash,
occlusion or continuous physical camera turn: check only that flagged interval and
record a specific disposition tied to the exact file. Detector failure is not a pass.
Scene detection is heuristic and cannot guarantee that every semantic state change
is found, so known multi-scene assets still need actual boundary selection.

The first Seedance travel sample exposed the failure: nominal 4/10/15/20-second
host boundaries differed from returned cuts at 4.0833/9.7083/15.25/20.4583 seconds;
the phone changed state at 5.7083 rather than the planned 6 seconds. Interleaving at
the nominal times left unwanted fragments. Keep this cause separate from image or
voice quality. The local correction uses the same complete native sources and adds
no generation charges.
