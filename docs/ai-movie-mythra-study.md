## Transferable decisions from film 1

1. Make the title's impossible premise visible early, inside an urgent human problem.
2. Give the protagonist a costly choice before large-scale spectacle. Attachment
   comes from chosen care, concrete feeding/protection and relinquished possessions.
3. Design a causal ladder: need → attempt → price → exposure → opposition → sacrifice
   → changed protagonist → consequence. Every sequence changes the next one's options.
4. Give the creature readable wants and responses. Scale should alter logistics,
   blocking, resources and concealment, not merely make the next image bigger.
5. Plant a small visual object or gesture and return to it with changed meaning.
   Invent original motifs; do not copy this ring, pink dragon, birth or grave story.
6. Build opposition through knowledge: what a witness sees, whom they tell, how a
   crowd forms. Wider framing can express social power without expository narration.
7. Reserve epic destruction for a character decision already made understandable.
   End on its emotional consequence, not another unrelated spectacle.
8. Improve on long repeated pleading/grief and blunt villainy when the new title
   benefits: retain essential emotional processing, but let each return disclose a
   new action, relationship change or fact. Do not claim shorter is automatically better.

## Five-aspect visual vocabulary

- **Subject:** recognizable mother, distinctive pink creature at successive sizes,
  repeated neighbors/priest/ally. Identity and costume carry continuity.
- **Subject Motion:** practical action and reaction: shelter, touch, feed, trade,
  hide, repair, restrain, flee, search, mourn, bury, retaliate. Avoid substituting
  slow posing for these verbs.
- **Scene:** snowy rocky valley, coherent stone-and-timber village, dim home,
  church, forest refuge and grave. Overlays are largely absent during action;
  title/end/credits are separate editorial layers.
- **Spatial Framing:** establishing wide for geography; medium relational two-shot
  for care/conflict; close face/eye/hand/prop for decisions; low creature view and
  high/wide village view for relative scale. Foreground doors/windows/rocks form
  readable thresholds between safety and danger.
- **Camera:** evidence includes changing scale/angle across cuts, deliberate reveals,
  low/eye/high viewpoints and shallow-focus portraits against deeper environment
  coverage. Exact focal lengths, rigs and every movement cannot be inferred from
  regular still samples; temporal-window observations below qualify specific motion.

## What the sequel adds to the reusable AI-movie style

- **A sequel begins with consequences.** The damaged village, dead people, surviving
  witnesses and grave persist. New story grows from those states instead of resetting.
- **Mystery lives in a concrete object and restricted behavior.** Who wrote the book,
  when and why? The audience sees enough to suspect; the daughter must act to learn.
- **Use conflicting relationships.** Florence's trust in her father conflicts with
  what the dragon does for her. Opposition costs identity and belonging, not only safety.
- **Turn a familiar care ritual into a suspense device.** The contaminated meal works
  because earlier feeding already means love. A new film should invent its own ritual.
- **Reveal knowledge in escalating steps.** Hidden book → secret writing → cruel
  orders → confession → public lie → origin revelation → physical true form.
- **Pay off social staging.** The hostile crowd becomes a protective circle around
  the creature after observable rescue. A belief change should follow evidence.
- **Give spectacle a rescue problem.** Who is falling, who can reach them, what blocks
  that rescue, and what is paid? Those questions make wide shots more than decoration.
- **Plant decisive world rules before they decide the ending.** The creator-death
  linkage is announced very late here; for our originals, plant such constraints early
  enough that the final price feels earned. Do not borrow a late rule merely for tears.
- **Scale to runtime.** The 64-minute film uses a long recap and repeated pleas,
  searches and conversations. Preserve the useful reversals; do not stretch a shorter
  brief by repeating the same emotional information. No retention data was available
  to establish whether those long passages helped or hurt this reference.
- **Keep the promised ending.** This film ends with reunion/grief and another return
  promise. OpenMontage should resolve the requested full movie's central problem;
  only use an unresolved serial ending when compatible with the brief.

## Lighting, performance, score and production implications

The strongest reusable visual contrast is **intimate care versus public threat**:
warm motivated domestic light; cold exterior exposure; monumental landscape/building
wide shots; close physical touch. The sequel extends this with candlelit secrecy,
blue night, bright eye/magic changes, pink versus black dragon silhouettes and
destruction that visibly alters familiar places. Preserve motivated light and
environmental progression; do not copy blue/orange grading onto every new genre.

Faces generally carry readable grief, surprise and tenderness, while hands, food,
doors and wounds externalize action. Several passages are broadly melodramatic;
the owner wants emotionally strong originals, not compulsory sobbing or continual
camera-facing line delivery. Plan a playable objective, restraint, reaction and
physical behavior for each performance. Exact voice quality was not audibly reviewed
by this agent; model-reported sound findings are separated below.

Captions repeatedly mark music, cries, breaths, snorts and speech; neither captions
nor waveform energy establish score quality or its precise arrangement. Therefore
the following is **our proposed application, not an observed soundtrack claim**:
write a small original attachment motif; leave dialogue space; use thin tension for
concealment, rhythmic pressure for pursuit, a selective orchestral expansion for the
earned climax, then stripped-back aftermath. Transform the motif's orchestration
when a relationship changes. Avoid permanent maximum intensity and copied melodies.
Use the existing authorized music-provider policy and budget.

Thirty-second generation packets can contain a meaningful miniature scene: an
objective, manageable action, reaction and changed state, covered in several useful
shots of at least two seconds. The films demonstrate frequent coverage changes and
held emotional beats, **not a need for thirty-second static holds**. Batch independent
packets using fixed cast/location/state references; retain causal order in the edit.
This study establishes reference principles, not measured Frameo throughput, an
accepted OpenMontage sample, guaranteed equivalent quality or guaranteed retention.

## Applying the study to useful 30-second multi-shot generations

Write the packet as one causally continuous scene, then specify coverage inside it.
Each shot needs subject, action, environment, framing and motivated camera behavior;
the packet also needs a shared starting state, locked geography/light/audio and an
explicit outgoing state. Five shots of 5/6/5/7/7 seconds are an example, not a quota.
Every actual internal shot must obey the owner's minimum of two seconds. Keep
complete native lines/actions inside the edit; a generator's nominal duration does
not guarantee compliant internal cuts.

**Original example — storm-damaged floodgate, 30 seconds:**

> One continuous late-afternoon scene at the same flooded stone gate. Cast lock:
> use the selected adult keeper and apprentice references, unchanged faces, voices,
> wet work clothes, dirt and injuries. Keeper begins screen-left at the jammed
> timber lever, apprentice screen-right beside the frayed safety rope. Floodwater
> moves rear-right to front-left; keep this direction across all shots. Storm sky
> remains the cool key, one sheltered amber work lantern stays frame-left; no
> weather/time jump. Gate is half shut, water leaks under its bottom edge, and the
> apprentice's right cuff is torn from the preceding scene. Start with both already
> braced against the current; do not reset them to a dry arrival.
>
> 0–5s: Medium-wide from the safe bank, both people and jammed lever in frame.
> Apprentice pulls the rope taut while keeper leans into the lever; it shifts only
> slightly. A modest forward camera move follows their effort and preserves the
> exit ladder behind the apprentice.
>
> 5–11s: Eye-level medium on keeper's working side, same screen direction. Keeper
> feels the lever stop, looks toward apprentice and says in the settled native
> voice, quietly and urgently, “Hold it there.” One playable breath and reaction;
> hands remain on the same grip. Water and wood strain continue beneath speech.
>
> 11–16s: Close detail from that same side shows the loaded wooden pin splitting
> and rope fibers separating. Maintain contact and force direction. Hold long
> enough to read the new damage; no flash insert, elastic timber or instant repair.
>
> 16–23s: Relational medium two-shot. Apprentice notices the split, takes one step
> toward the visible ladder and stops when keeper's footing slips. Keeper looks
> from escape route to rising water; their eye lines establish the costly choice.
> Camera stays steady enough to understand body support and distance.
>
> 23–30s: Wider side view. Apprentice abandons the exit, secures the rope around
> the established stone post and helps keeper regain footing. Gate drops one
> handspan with a heavy wooden impact; hold the changed water flow and their
> exhausted reaction. End with keeper still holding the lever, apprentice gripping
> the newly secured rope, pin visibly cracked, ladder still inaccessible.
>
> Native audio: sustained flowing water, breath and clothing, rope/wood strain,
> one complete spoken line, then gate impact; preserve coherent outdoor space.
> If music is approved, use a thin original tension cue under speech with room for
> the impact; do not invent a loud new cue at each cut. No narration, title or text
> overlays. Outgoing state becomes the next packet's exact starting condition.

The next packet must solve the still-cracked pin and blocked escape, not restart
the same pulling action. Parallelize other independent prepared sequences; keep
actual same-shot continuation dependencies sequential and use the outgoing frame
where supported. This is preparation detail, not a request for new routine paid
review, per-clip assessment or an additional approval stage.
