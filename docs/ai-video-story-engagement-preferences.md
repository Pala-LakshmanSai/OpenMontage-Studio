# Extreme, surprising stories planned before generation

Owner correction, 3 November 2026: the Glacier film's story was still too mild.
General promises of "extreme stakes" are insufficient. For narrative AI videos,
design a much bolder, highly engaging story during screenplay and scene planning,
including surprising threats such as an animal approaching the host when the
setting supports it. Save and apply this training from the project on every
device, alongside the active style and [global realism](ai-video-global-realism-preferences.md).

## Build the hook and escalation into the story

Open with a short, strong, title-faithful hook: a compelling visible situation,
clear stakes and an urgent unresolved question. Bring consequential trouble into
the opening sequence and an extreme situation into the opening few minutes where
the premise supports it. Do not spend a long introduction merely announcing the
trip or hide all meaningful danger late in the film. Preserve understandable
chronology; a later-event preview must be legible as a preview.

For a long film, plan several distinct major turns with rising consequences and
surprises throughout. Go beyond ordinary chores, mild inconvenience and repeated
warnings. In a survival story, credible near-death danger, a threatening animal,
an exit becoming unusable or a costly failed escape can fundamentally change the
plan. Other narrative styles need equally ambitious conflicts appropriate to
their title and genre. A louder sound, another reaction or the same setback in a
new angle is not a new dramatic turn.

Connect the turns: establish a cause or clue, reveal the unexpected problem,
force a difficult choice, show the attempt, make its cost persist, then earn
relief or a changed method. A surprise should change the route, time available,
equipment, shelter, objective or relationship to a threat. Practical work, meals
and rest remain useful when they advance that problem or deliver earned relief;
do not let them become an interchangeable twenty-minute routine.

## Make surprises consequential and believable

Choose threats creatively at the premise level, including events that would be
impractical or too dangerous for a real personality channel to stage. Keep the
invented action physically credible and visibly understandable. Do not copy a
reference creator's incidents, face, voice or dialogue, or promise retention
metrics. Reference rawness and pacing do not require limiting the fictional
story to that creator's real-world production constraints.

A wildlife encounter needs a compatible species, habitat, season, behavior and
route. Seed signs when useful, establish the animal's distance and approach, show
the host noticing it and making a consequential decision, and carry the changed
access, lost time or abandoned equipment forward. It must be an actual story
sequence, not a disconnected animal cutaway followed by unchanged camp chores.
Do not automatically insert an animal into every film or invent implausible
species mixtures. Uncertainty about a factual environment is resolved before
committing that fact to the plan.

Extreme does not mean arbitrary disasters, magical rescues, invulnerable people,
elastic geography or resetting a lost object. Preserve selected identity, camera
ownership, within-day weather and light, ordinary human movement and each style's
source, art, voice and sound rules. Factual/source-led documentaries must not
present invented danger as evidence; non-narrative or explicitly gentle briefs
retain their purpose.

## Record an actionable plan once

Put the engagement plan into the existing script and scene-plan artifacts, for
example `metadata.story_engagement`; no new schema, separate review service or
approval stage is required. For each major turn record:

- **Hook or open question:** what the viewer immediately needs to understand.
- **Seed and cause:** the earlier condition, choice or clue that makes it possible.
- **Surprise and stakes:** what changes, what could be lost, and why it matters now.
- **Agency and attempt:** the difficult choice and the physical action taken.
- **Cost and persistent state:** what cannot reset in the following scenes.
- **Payoff and scene IDs:** the visible result and the exact shots that prove it.

Give the major set pieces useful source time and complete actions, not a token
one-second flash. Plan normal-speed action, varied useful coverage, clear spatial
blocking and complete native speech. Avoid giving every unit the same calm,
unhurried, four-step cadence when the dramatic situation calls for urgency.

This is upfront writing and staging within the existing
[fast workflow](video-fast-production-preferences.md). Reuse settled research,
prepare frozen independent sequences in parallel and preserve accepted jobs.
Routine paid review/scoring remains USD 0; this correction adds no repeated
audition, per-clip assessment, review-score wait or voice-selection gate. Once
production is authorized, continue within the actual budget and provider rules.
