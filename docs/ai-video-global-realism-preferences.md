# Global realism and progression for AI videos

## Title-led extreme stakes and earned progression

Follow [extreme story and surprise planning](ai-video-story-engagement-preferences.md). The owner rejected the delivered Glacier story as too mild on 3 November 2026; generic extreme-stakes wording is not enough. Plan bolder distinct threats and consequential surprises before generation, including habitat-appropriate wildlife where the story supports it.

Owner refinement, 3 November 2026: build the engagement into the story and
screenplay before generating footage. Across narrative AI styles, pursue highly
creative, extreme situations rather than a routine sequence of attractive but
uneventful tasks. Where the title and genre support it, use a severe survival
crisis, imminent loss or credible near-death danger that a real personality
channel could not responsibly stage. Establish a short, strong title-faithful
hook and introduce consequential trouble early; a full seven-day journey still
begins at Day 1, with any later-event preview clearly legible as a preview.

Escalate through a visible cause, a difficult decision, a physical attempt, a
setback or cost, a changed method and an earned payoff. Carry injuries, fatigue,
lost equipment, damaged shelter, weather and unfinished work forward when the
story establishes them. Give relief and practical progress enough space to make
the next threat matter. Extreme ambition is not permission for random unrelated
disasters, magical rescues, impossible movement or invisible construction.
Ground the action in believable materials, forces, geography and human limits;
retain the current style's staging, camera, voice, art and sound treatment.

These are fictional storytelling preferences, not claims about reference
creators, real incidents or guaranteed retention. Match the supplied references'
documented storytelling and rawness without cloning a creator's identity or
copying their scenes. Factual/source-led documentaries must not invent events
as evidence, and a non-narrative or specifically gentle brief retains its own
purpose; use the strongest appropriate conflict rather than inserting danger
that contradicts the requested film. Save new reusable corrections through
[portable production knowledge](portable-production-knowledge.md).

Owner refinement, 3 November 2026: apply [human voice and facial performance](ai-video-human-performance-preferences.md) for generated people/speech. Reuse selected voices and clean references, prepare speakable lines and achievable facial/camera/light staging once, and use premium footage selectively within the authorized scope/budget. No new review or approval stage; routine paid reviews remain USD 0. Preserve no-narrator/no-speech styles, stylized art, supplied original audio, native pairing and the current autonomous voice-selection policy (no routine voice-approval gate).

## No flash clips or next-scene fragments

Apply [minimum shot duration for every style](minimum-shot-duration-preferences.md).
Ordinary shots must last at least 2 seconds unless explicitly requested otherwise.
Use actual generated scene boundaries and meaningful screen states, not nominal
prompt timings. Preserve complete speech when repairing a fragment. Run the local
cached short-shot check; no extra paid review is needed.

## Text belongs inside the scene

Apply [text on screens and physical surfaces](in-scene-text-preferences.md) across
all styles. Phone/status/board/ticket information must be readable on its actual
surface, with appropriate perspective, movement and occlusion. Do not substitute
or duplicate it with a floating lower third. The owner rejected that treatment
in the first-class sample on 2 November 2026. Avoid unrequested scene labels.

Plan and make each video feel convincing and real throughout, rather than visibly
artificial. Take the same care with story development, screenplay, staging,
performance, camera, sound, editing and the final moving image. Ground plausible
physical and environmental details in appropriate references; distinguish a
fictional reconstruction from independently verified facts. Don't claim that a
photorealistic prompt or a technically valid render guarantees realism.

Establish an explicit initial world and keep its development causal. The setting,
people, objects, wardrobe, tools, weather, light, condition and accumulated work
must change only through visible action or a clear, plausible passage of time.
Use the starting condition appropriate to the story: for a shelter being built,
show its raw, unfinished, messy state first, then show each meaningful improvement
and its consequences. Do not begin with a finished shelter and imply it is being
built, or allow props and surfaces to reset, appear, disappear or transform between
shots. An already clean or new object may remain clean or new when the story calls
for it; artificial grime and arbitrary disorder are not realism.

When an owner has selected or approved a character's identity, carry that identity
into new samples, restarts and scratch rebuilds unless the owner explicitly changes
the casting. A scratch rebuild can use fresh scenes and footage while retaining the
selected identity reference; it does not silently authorize a different face or
character. Preserve natural skin and believable age and check the character in
actual motion as well as reference images. Recorded selection does not itself prove
that every generated face matches.

Build this progression into the script, shot/state ledger, anchor reviews and
actual edit before generating dependent scenes. Ground speech or narration in the
actions, observations and elapsed events that the viewer can see; do not describe
work, change or consequences that have not occurred or contradict the footage.
Preserve motivated camera ownership, geography, contact, action order, natural
timing, believable light and sound, and physical material behavior. Accepted moving
footage determines later state, not a planned outcome the model did not show.

## Time, weather and environmental continuity

Plan the location, season, time of day, weather, temperature, light and sound as
one connected environmental state. Track changes within a single scene and day,
including between adjacent takes, as well as across larger time jumps. Record the
cloud cover and haze; sun position, direction and height; light intensity and color;
shadow direction and length; wind, precipitation, snow and wetness; and their
effects on surfaces, clothing, people, props and ambience when relevant. Interior conditions must be motivated by the
space and sources; an outdoor temperature does not establish the indoor one.

Make each change physically and narratively plausible: daylight and shadows follow
the passing time, and weather changes through observable development or a clear
time transition. Maintain the established state across a continuous action unless
the scene actually shows a change. Avoid abrupt sunny-to-stormy resets, conflicting
shadow directions, sudden snowfall or wet/dry changes without cause, and sound that
contradicts the visible conditions. A deliberate and legible passage of time can
change conditions; do not force a day/night or weather change when the story does
not need one. Use research and real-world references as appropriate, and distinguish
inferred or fictional conditions from verified observations.

Use verified local seasonal and daylight context where factual precision matters;
describe unknown weather as a fictional choice rather than an invented forecast.
Review neighboring anchors before animation, then inspect moving takes, camera
reverses and joins for unexplained environmental changes.

Review the complete sequence, not only isolated attractive images. Check human
skin and eyes, anatomy, acting, material texture, tool contact, movement, lighting,
prop positions, accumulated work, transitions, dialogue and sound over time. Reject
artificial polishing, improbable physical changes, unexplained resets, or generated
artifacts instead of concealing them with unrelated cuts, grain or other effects.
Resolve issues with justified targeted paid corrections when needed, within the
user's actual budget and authorized scope. Preserve receipts and reconcile unknown
jobs before any resubmission. When a required fix exceeds a user-set budget or
requires an unauthorized purchase, preserve work and explain the specific blocker.
Report honestly which subjective or
audiovisual checks could not be performed. This preference expresses the desired
quality, not proof of acceptance or a guarantee that every generation will pass.

## Shot joins and scale continuity — owner correction, 2 November 2026

Apply this continuity preference to all future owner videos and their neighboring
shots, while preserving each workflow's source and creative treatment. Avoid
unexplained zoom-in/zoom-out impressions, scale jumps, elastic geometry and camera
movement resets that make a continuous experience feel like separate clips. Track
lens/focal length, camera distance and height, framing/headroom, movement direction
and speed, perspective, character/prop state, lighting and sound across each join.
Intentional location, time or camera changes remain possible when clearly motivated.
For generated same-shot continuations, extract the exact final usable frame at the
actual chosen edit point and use it as the next starting reference where supported;
a different source end frame does not establish the selected edit's continuity.
Inspect the actual moving result and at least two seconds on both sides of joins,
with dense samples near any fault. Frame matching alone does not prevent the next
generated take from reframing or changing scale. Keep constant aspect-preserving
transforms and SAR; do not conceal defects with digital zoom, stretching, morphs,
or interpolation. This does not turn real-footage documentaries into AI workflows
or prohibit purposeful editorial cuts.

For a doorway, vehicle, collapse or other barrier, specify the camera's side and
the character's side independently in every planned shot. State which crossing
has actually occurred; a new angle must not move the person across the barrier.
A long multi-shot generation can still reset this state. If a concrete returned
fault is found, preserve complete useful native shots and repair the local edit
before buying a replacement; this adds no routine per-clip review requirement.
Observed 3 November 2026: the Glacier Frameo collapse included shots with the host
already outside. The complete host-free fall/impact/settling shot was retained,
and the incorrect host shots omitted without regeneration. Evidence stays with
that production's `production/assembly-repair-FG24/local-selection-proof.json`.

Name each reference's role in the prompt. A character portrait supplies identity;
its background, cargo and earlier equipment do not establish the current scene.
Prepare clean identity and location references before a limited generation window
where possible, and state the current prop inventory separately. Observed in the
same Glacier production: a late route shot reintroduced two loaded sleds after the
camp was abandoned. Its complete clean crevasse POV was kept and the contradictory
wide shots omitted locally; see `production/assembly-repair-FG38/`. Preserve lost
equipment as lost rather than allowing reference-background objects to return.
