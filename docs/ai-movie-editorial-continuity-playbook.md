## What failed and what it teaches

The reviewed export was playable and its detected shots exceeded two seconds, yet
the owner rejected abrupt cuts, continuity and the ending. Full-timeline shot
samples and selected temporal excerpts exposed invented cast, role duplication,
restored injuries/possessions, changing animal scale, vehicle-layout changes,
missing causal actions and a post-ending scene joined directly to the reunion.
Most runtime came from entire source containers. Attractive individual pictures,
successful jobs, a detailed prompt, sufficient duration and stable audio sample
counts did not establish a coherent movie.

These failures require better scene design, compatible reference states and real
editing. They are not solved by more realism adjectives, grain, tighter crops,
longer minimum shots, dissolves everywhere or another automatic review service.
Keep successful patterns: clear relationships in one frame, motivated practical
light, tactile materials, voluntary choices before trust, legible clue→recognition
coverage and actions whose physical result actually appears.

## 1. Write the audience's discovery, not a list of clip IDs

Start with the complete causal story and ending. Each dramatic sequence changes
what the audience knows, fears or hopes, and changes what the protagonist can do.
Show the cause, consequential attempt, result and reaction needed to understand
that change. Decide whether a revelation belongs first to the character or the
audience. An accidental early wide can spend a mystery before the character earns
the vantage point; repeating the same reveal afterward is not escalation.

Use the existing beat map for these choices. Source timestamps and 30-second
generation units are planning estimates, not mandatory scene boundaries. Prefer
useful 30-second requests when appropriate, but let action complexity and speech
determine shot count and take length. Do not impose the same four-shot cadence on
every situation or make a character wait to fill the remaining seconds.

Apply [complete endings and flexible runtime](ai-video-story-engagement-preferences.md#complete-endings-and-flexible-runtime--owner-correction-4-november-2026).
Protect decision, cost, aftermath, relationship payoff and a performed emotional
landing. Complete the final picture and sound before any clearly separate stinger.
Approximate runtime may flex; actual budget, pass expiry and explicit hard limits
remain binding. Do not invent filler or add a fade over missing closure.

## 2. Carry the world, not just the face

Maintain one current state in the existing bible/sequence artifacts. Include only
relevant facts in the model request; do not paste the whole bible into every shot.
Irreversible events persist until an authored cause changes them: a sold possession
is gone, a depleted resource stays depleted, a shortened body part stays shortened,
a disabled person stays in the established supported position, and a dead character
does not return as a standing extra. Temporary natural regrowth needs its own cause
and legible before/after, rather than silently resetting the silhouette.

Lock nonhuman individuals as carefully as humans: anatomy/gait, coat or scale map,
eye/frill/ear shape, marks, growth stage and current injury. Use consistent ratios
to people, furniture and vehicles. A numerical size or "pony-sized" adjective does
not fix a baby reference dominating the picture. Establish a giant and a human in
one coherent scale master; derive close and wide coverage from that geometry instead
of shrinking the creature to fit a convenient two-shot.

For carried or transferred story objects, carry both custody and support through
the actual action: who has the object, what contains or supports it, which hand is
free, and where it ends. A closed case on another person's lap is not evidence of
stowage on the protagonist. A deliberate packing/travel ellipsis can bridge
compatible visible states; it cannot excuse an impossible location or invented
handoff. Treat old background props in references as a possible source of resets.

Story-bearing quantities and background dressing obey the same rule. A scarcity
scene must not acquire shelves of visually identical full supplies. Separate a
selected person's identity from jewelry, equipment and old background inventory.
Use a compatible current-state master once at a significant change, or crop a clean
identity reference so it does not contradict that state. Textual role labels do not
neutralize conflicting pixels. Keep clean identity roots for independent setups;
use an actual outgoing edit frame for a genuine continuation where supported.

## 3. Stage roles, routes and functional geography

Specify the visible cast for each shot, with distinct roles and appearances.
Several uniformed people must not inherit the same lead identity. Keep offscreen
participants where the preceding action put them; do not render a duplicate just
because another role needs to hold a child or open a door. Reduce an overcrowded
composition or divide its actions rather than asking one shot to solve everything.

Freeze a simple geography for an interacting sequence: doors, support surfaces,
seats, steering wheel, bed versus trailer, restraint, exits and travel direction.
A reverse angle changes the view, not who occupies the driver's seat or which
vehicle carries the subject. Show a transfer, replacement or move when the story
needs it; otherwise preserve the same object and location. An intentional time or
place ellipsis needs a legible new situation, not a contradictory mid-action setup.

When communication, travel or recovery requires a substantial delay, make the
necessary elapsed-time cue literal and reconcile its origin with the final edit.
An immediate cut to a distant response must not imply impossible instant travel
or communication. Keep recorded material inside its established physical receiver
or screen; a broadcast reaction does not put that person in the protagonist's room.

For consequential mechanics use a readable sequence: notice/orient → approach or
reach → supported contact → operation → visible result → response. An already
spilled bottle does not show the impact that caused the loss; an apology does not
by itself show the resulting injury. Offscreen action is legitimate when deliberately
made clear by an appropriate cue and consequence, not a default hiding place for
missing causal work. Give difficult interactions one manageable action and clean
coverage, with safe, non-graphic presentation when needed.

## 4. Direct a camera that explains the action

Apply the [whole-frame performance rule](ai-video-action-clarity-and-pacing.md#direct-everyone-visible-including-the-silent-people).
The active scene continues for every relevant person, not only the lead or speaker.
Retain ongoing tasks, listener reactions, emotional progression, posture and support
across coverage. A parent silently witnessing an apparent loss needs a readable
response consistent with what they know; a shocked freeze is not an unlimited
neutral hold. Avoid unshown standing/kneeling/seated resets. Camera activity cannot
replace meaningful acting; trim inactive tails rather than prolong them for scale.

Choose an establishing view for geography, relational coverage for a choice/contact,
and detail for a clue or recognition. Lens names alone are insufficient. State the
camera's position, height and side relative to the interaction; preserve eyelines,
screen direction, supports and key light across reverse angles. A close-up of a
prone person must retain the established ground plane rather than inheriting a
portrait wall. Keep the same scale anchors and enough clearance for the action.

Change angle, distance or move because it reveals useful information or changes
emotional distance. Avoid near-identical framing with a reset pose, convenient
relocation or new furniture at the cut. For continuous movement, preserve its phase
and velocity into compatible coverage. Cross the interaction axis only with a
deliberate reorientation, crossing move or clear neutral geography. Naturalistic
camera work need not move all the time, and not every cut needs a transition effect.

## 5. Make the edit from actual performances

The editor owns the film; the provider owns only returned coverage. Publish usable
ranges while generation continues, but distinguish available/downloaded footage
from an editorial selection. Do not build a final timeline by automatically taking
every ordered source from frame zero to its last frame. A complete take can be the
best choice; retain it for its dramatic work, not its paid or included duration.

In the existing sequence/edit metadata record the intended entry, useful action or
exchange, exit state and reason for the next cut. Select around an action completion,
new information, a look/response or an intentional ellipsis. Cut repetitive setup,
waiting, duplicate revelation and decorative aftermath while retaining complete
native utterances and ordinary actual shots of at least two seconds. The minimum
duration is a floor, not evidence that a cut is motivated.

Resolve incompatible state at its cause: correct an unsubmitted binding, use a
compatible alternative range or make a bounded justified pickup within authority.
A dissolve cannot reconcile a seated passenger with the next shot's driver, or a
fallen character with the same person standing elsewhere. A crop is useful only
if the resulting shot still communicates its intended action; do not use it to
claim unseen contact, correct identity or an outcome that never occurred.

For a concrete peripheral prop, background or reset fault, a fixed source-aware
reframe or a trim at an actual shot boundary may retain the useful performance.
Choose it for the resulting composition and story purpose, not merely to conceal
pixels. Keep the decisive action, readable physical screen and necessary context;
do not use arbitrary moving crops or zooms. Preserve originals and record exact
selected ranges/transforms. If a clue or payoff is excluded, mark it unverified or
adapt the beat honestly; hidden content is not proof that the generated state was
correct. Preserve ordinary actual shots of at least two seconds and complete words.

A locally successful repair must still join the actual neighboring world. Derive
its anchor from the established support, apparatus, blocking and camera side;
change the diagnosed feature without silently replacing the set or carrier.
Reconcile the repaired entry and exit with adjacent footage once during the
targeted repair. If those neighbors already contain the wrong persistent state,
include their affected coverage in the fix rather than reattaching it unchanged.
Do not declare a sequence repaired solely because the replacement works alone.

## 6. Keep performance, speech, text and sound truthful

Separate exact spoken lines from action/camera instructions in the generation
request. Name each speaker and its permitted mouth window. Do not place staging
prose, markup or explanations inside dialogue. Avoid future-state wording or
references that reveal a concealed transformation early. Preserve native mouth/word
offsets when trimming; ASR can suggest words but does not prove speaker identity,
voice quality or phoneme accuracy.

Reuse suitable settled native voices and clean supported reference crops through
planned useful performances. Give each line and its physical action enough
performed time; do not stretch a short exchange to fill the request. Preserve
native speech-picture pairs through local repairs. Derive trims, concatenation
and display timing from the selected picture frames and timestamped native PCM
sample clock, retaining complete words and effect tails rather than sliding or
replacing speech. When a picture-only repair leaves the selected audio unchanged,
reuse its verified identity and cached checks instead of repeating ASR or analysis.
If important spoken evidence is unresolved, one cached batch transcript of the
selected native audio without added score can support the existing check; ASR
uncertainty is not a missing-line verdict or speaker/lip-quality certification.

When a gesture carries the story's meaning, preserve its actual target, order and
readable contact. Reaching toward another face is not equivalent to tapping one's
own chest. Stage a feasible gesture for that anatomy, with enough framing to read
the intended response; do not infer success from a generic raised paw or hand.

Plot-critical writing must be readable as the intended word on its physical
surface. A writing gesture plus garbled glyphs does not pay off a naming or refusal
beat. Prefer simple supported text and clean coverage; if necessary, repair that
surface locally with matching perspective/occlusion rather than add a floating
caption. Incidental text does not need a new typography review workflow.

Plan a location's sound continuity with the scene. Keep native dialogue, contact
sounds and meaningful effect tails. Use a motivated pre-lap, outgoing tail, listener
view or continuous compatible ambience when it helps a scene cross a boundary;
an intentional location/time cut can change sound. Do not slide visible speech
against its mouth, loop contaminated dialogue as ambience or put music everywhere
to cover abrupt joins. Preserve no-music/no-narrator and source-audio-only styles.

## 7. Use the existing fast workflow honestly

Before each ready sequence, fill the existing contract rather than create a new
ledger: narrative change/evidence; visible cast and current world state; action
route; camera/coverage; exact lines and sound; entry/exit and next-cut intent. Bind
compatible references, freeze ready work and keep independent production moving.
Only actual continuation dependencies wait for returned state. Design ambitious
story turns with simpler readable staging, not a longer stack of instructions.

While selecting/conforming, treat conspicuous violations of those story-bearing
facts as faults rather than accepting them because the job succeeded. Keep accepted
and uncertain receipts; no blind replay. In the one brief final check, use moving
opening, consequential interaction/transition and ending passages, plus required
text/state evidence when material. Reuse cached local cuts and technical/audio
checks. A still frame, minimum-shot pass and valid file cannot certify all joins.

Include the full composition and edges in those selected moving passages, especially
the ending: incidental figures, an old possession restored too early or a wrong
prop can survive otherwise useful close coverage. Check the final encoded repair
in its neighboring action once. In the accepted Mars delivery, this caught concrete
ending prop/figure contradictions; fixed purposeful views retained the family,
public consequence and departure performances without changing native audio.
The resulting edit does not claim the excluded prop-placement action was visible.
This is part of the same brief final check, not a second full-film review.

The detailed whole-timeline review that produced these lessons was explicitly
requested by the owner after rejection. It is not a recurring production stage.
No automatic paid critic, every-join audit, per-clip ASR, quality score, audition,
sample-before-sample or new approval gate is added. Report unresolved material
faults and owner acceptance separately from technical delivery; do not promise
error-free output or identical results on a friend's hardware.
