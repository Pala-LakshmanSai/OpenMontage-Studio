# Human voice and facial performance without slower production

Owner instruction, 3 November 2026. Apply across current and future AI-video
styles when generated people or speech are present. Improve the approved main
route before paying for a premium model. This is a preparation and reuse policy,
not another review, audition or approval stage. Follow the current
[voice policy](ai-video-voice-selection-preferences.md): reuse a suitable settled
voice, otherwise select an appropriate consistent native voice autonomously.
Do not pause for routine voice options or approval; use a planned useful dialogue
take and preserve explicitly requested choices and style speech defaults.

Preserve the style: an observational film stays without an added narrator or
dialogue; a stylized character retains its intended art rather than becoming
photorealistic. Do not replace supplied original audio or alter real footage.
Keep the selected cast, native speech-picture pairing, continuity, actual budget,
minimum shot duration, provider authorization and accepted-job/no-replay rules.

## Prepare once and reuse

For physical acting, follow the global realism guide's owner correction on
attention, contact and correct control/prop use. The actor looks/orients toward
the target before reaching, uses the designed grip/turn/press/lever motion and
receives the visible physical result. Do not reach at one object while staring
unmotivatedly at another, press a rotary knob as an invented switch, touch active
unguarded machinery, or treat a wrong background control as the intended device.
Apply force, weight, support, resistance, recoil and injury reactions coherently.
Put this directly in the existing action/framing/reference entry and simplify
unnecessary choreography; it adds no routine audition, paid review or gate.

Keep a compact performance entry in the existing character bible and shot plan,
not a separate workflow. Record the selected voice ID or supported reference,
accent, register, conversational delivery, portrait master, and baseline framing.
Per speaking beat add the exact words, intention/listener, one main action,
plausible speech duration with reaction room, light source and camera path.
Reuse those values; change only what the scene motivates.

Identity and wardrobe apply to every shot that could reveal the traveler, not
only rows marked as visible speech. An offscreen-narration or POV shot can turn
and expose a face, body or sleeve. Carry the approved identity reference and
clothing into those potential reveals, or stage the camera so the person stays
physically out of view. Keep distinct supporting roles explicit; a driver is
not a replacement host. Check the returned reveal itself across the selected
movement rather than validating only the following selfie. This is a targeted
continuity check for the planned action, not another paid review or global gate.
Owner correction, 3 November 2026: an all-Max taxi-entry shot revealed a different
traveler and outfit; the owner declined repair of that delivered sample. Do not
reuse that faulty footage or treat its earlier technical delivery as acceptance.

### Voice material and selection

- Prefer the already selected voice and clean, expressive material that sounds
  like this character speaking to a person. A catalog label, age or accent does
  not establish naturalness. For an unresolved choice, use cached catalog
  previews and a small shortlist; any authorized audition can be the first useful
  story line, not a disposable multi-provider contest.
- When reference audio is supported, prepare one clean, continuous excerpt once.
  Roughly 6–10 seconds is a practical starting length, not a measured optimum or
  universal contract. Include a complete phrase with natural stress and pauses;
  avoid music, other voices, clipping and long silence. Preserve the original
  file and cache the validated crop/upload. Meet the actual route's limits.
- Reuse the approved identity and voice guidance for independent scenes. A clean
  accepted native performance may become a reference when it matches the selected
  voice; do not recursively learn from progressively degraded output or silently
  change the voice. Narration and native speech must represent the same character
  while retaining plausible room/microphone differences. Reference conditioning
  guides performance; it does not guarantee an exact timbre match.
- Verify the local request actually carries the supported reference field on the
  first setup or after a contract change. Never claim conditioning from a local
  filename that was not uploaded or from a soundtrack-replacement parameter.

### Speakable writing and direction

- Write contractions, concrete observations and complete thought groups. Match
  the character's purpose and listener: confiding, deciding, disappointed,
  breathless from work, or relieved. Give one concise intention, not a stack of
  incompatible emotions. A crisis can require urgency; do not force every line
  into the same calm cadence.
- Use ordinary punctuation and scene context. Resolve difficult names, numbers
  and abbreviations in the spoken script before generating. Do not sprinkle
  artificial stutters, sighs, chuckles, breaths or fillers into every sentence.
  Do not put delivery instructions in spoken text unless the exact TTS model
  supports them; tags can be spoken literally.
- Estimate timing from the selected reference or existing accepted delivery.
  Leave time for the complete line and a natural reaction; simplify an overloaded
  sentence before submission rather than force fast speech or stretch audio.
  Do not impose identical word quotas or cut every thought into tiny fragments.
- For narration, generate useful paragraph/scene groups rather than resetting
  the voice sentence by sentence. For visible speech, preserve jointly generated
  native sound and picture and select actual complete utterances. Never paste
  independently timed TTS over a moving mouth and call it synchronized.

### Lip sync and natural changes of pace

Owner correction, 3 November 2026: the delivered Frameo travel film was rejected
for lip-sync errors in many places and an artificial, uniformly slow delivery.
Apply this correction to **every current and future AI video style with speech**,
through this shared project guide. Preserve no-narrator/no-speech styles.

- Lock voice identity (person, accent, register and recognizable timbre), not one
  delivery speed or emotional cadence for the whole film. Do not paste a global
  "unhurried", "slow", "relaxed" or intimate-read direction into every scene.
  Give each beat a situation, listener, intention and natural pace: a quick
  practical decision, a spontaneous relieved reaction, a quieter tired thought,
  an observant explanation or a more urgent short burst. Allow changes within a
  thought group, emphasis and pauses caused by thinking or action. Avoid a
  repeated pause after every short sentence, fake breath/filler quotas and random
  speed changes that are unrelated to the situation.
- Do not stretch every paragraph across the same generator duration or reuse
  identical word counts, speaking deadlines and shot grids. A long generation
  can contain several useful actions and shots; its length is not a requirement
  to keep the voice speaking slowly until the final second. Write speakable
  thought groups first, allow their natural duration, then stage useful action,
  reaction or scene sound around them. Choose actual usable edit points after
  the words are complete. Keep the requested finished-film length in mind.
  If a model dilates an ordinary phrase to fill a long envelope despite the
  direction, choose a shorter performance duration from its meaningful word
  count and situation, with room for the physical turn/reaction. This is a
  quality exception to an unlimited plan's 30-second default. Alternatively
  pack genuinely useful additional content into a long request; do not add
  filler or accelerate the completed AV to compensate. The first travel repair
  made one 22-word phrase last 12.22 seconds versus 7.84 seconds in its source,
  so longer duration and more adjectives were not themselves a pacing fix.
- Give every speaking shot an explicit speaker and exact local utterance. Bind
  phrase IDs to the visible mouth and state when speech starts and stops in that
  shot. A global paragraph plus "native lip sync" is insufficient when the face
  enters, leaves or returns midway through a multi-shot generation. In a
  handheld host-to-view turn, finish the on-camera phrase before turning, allow
  the physical turn, and start any offscreen continuation once the mouth is out
  of view. At a return, bring the face back before a new complete on-camera
  phrase begins. Do not ask the model to guess which words belong to each face
  window. Avoid an action, camera turn and demanding spoken phrase competing at
  the same moment.
- Keep narration and visible dialogue distinct in the plan while retaining the
  selected character voice. Narration may accompany appropriate non-speaking
  footage. Visible dialogue must retain its actual synchronized picture/audio
  pair, or use a specifically authorized and verified lip-sync route. Replacing
  the soundtrack beneath an already speaking face is not a sync repair. Do not
  hide a missing required host performance by quietly changing the film into
  offscreen narration.
- Native audio, a matched voice reference, valid streams, a decoded export and
  matching nominal durations do **not** establish mouth-to-word synchronization.
  Use the first already planned useful host performance to check a clear spoken
  phrase against the moving mouth, including an early and later part and any
  face-return boundary, before copying its speech/staging pattern across a
  batch. Check actual consonant closures and open vowels against the heard or
  reliably aligned words; a still frame or waveform alone cannot pass this
  check. Independent nondependent footage can continue in parallel. This is a
  content check inside the existing fast workflow, not an audition contest,
  paid scoring loop or owner-approval gate.
- On an actual reported lip-sync fault, compare the original generated take,
  conformed selection and final timeline. Separate a constant offset or drift
  introduced by editing from incorrect articulation already present in the
  source. Preserve presentation timing during frame-rate conversion and native
  AV joins. Correct a measured offset locally when justified; replace or repair
  affected native performances when the mouth itself is wrong. Check the repaired
  moving-mouth content and its final joins before calling the problem fixed.
  Do not globally speed up, slow down or pitch-shift the finished film as a
  substitute for natural performance or use an arbitrary audio delay to mask
  phonemes that do not match.

Prompt evidence from the rejected travel run: all 50 submitted 26-second requests
used one 8/9/9-second visual grid and a blanket end-by-24-seconds speech rule;
21 host/hybrid requests bound a global paragraph to changing face visibility
without shot-local phrases. Repeated calm voice direction and fixed timing are
credible mechanisms for monotony and underconstrained articulation, not proof
that every observed fault had the same cause. The separate timing investigation
confirmed an export defect: linear sample decoding of concatenated AAC retained
join padding without honoring packet timing, adding about 40.667 ms per join and
roughly 2.014 seconds of voice lag by chunk 50. Source and native-concatenated
timing did not show that progressive drift. Use the shared timestamp-aware PCM
preparation in `lib/native_audio_assembly.py` before sample-based music mixing;
do not linearly decode the copied AAC concat and assume it remains aligned.
The focused regression checks marker timing across multiple joins. This fixes
the measured assembly mechanism; it does not certify original mouth shapes or
make speech natural. These changes reduce avoidable recurrence;
generative lip sync and perceived naturalness must still be observed in the
returned performance and cannot be guaranteed by prompting alone.

### Facial staging and movement

- Use the selected clean identity master for independent beats; use an exact
  outgoing edit frame for a genuinely continuous action where supported. Do not
  keep chaining a face through unrelated scenes. Retain natural age and texture;
  requesting more pores, wrinkles or sharpening is not a repair for plastic skin.
- Stage routine dialogue at a believable camera distance, often a medium or
  medium-close view, near eye level with the face away from extreme frame edges.
  Avoid unnecessarily close wide-angle selfies, low under-chin angles and rapid
  perspective changes. This is a risk-reduction choice, not a ban on motivated
  close-ups, profiles or expressive acting.
- Establish a plausible window, sky or practical light that exposes the face
  without flattened highlights or unmotivated underlighting. Where the scene
  permits, reposition the character/camera relative to existing light rather
  than inventing a new light, time of day or weather. Maintain shadow direction,
  exposure and color across continuous coverage. Keep natural shadows and age.
- Specify a natural expression tied to the beat and allow ordinary blinks and
  small gaze changes; avoid a permanent smile, fixed stare, exaggerated brows or
  repetitive nodding. Prefer positive, achievable direction over long lists of
  realism adjectives and prohibitions.
- During a demanding spoken line, use one main action and a coherent gentle
  camera move. Place complicated prop handling or a large turn at a natural
  clause/reaction boundary when possible. Preserve each style's physical turns,
  real-time action and camera ownership; do not freeze the person or remove
  lively environmental motion to make a face easier.
- Do not use face restoration, beauty smoothing, heavy denoising, synthetic
  pitch variation, sharpen/grain overlays or motion interpolation as a generic
  humanizing pass. Correct a concrete technical fault only with an appropriate
  local treatment, preserving texture, consonants and performance.

## Provider controls and selective premium footage

The registered H3 Max reference-to-video route supports actual audio reference
URLs. Turbo lacks that reference route; its target_audio_url replaces the
soundtrack and does not create voice conditioning or establish lip sync. J1TTS's
current adapter exposes text/voice selection, not arbitrary stability, emotion
or speed knobs. Do not copy another model's tags, SSML or controls into it.
Recheck the live route when its contract changes.

Seedance or another authorized premium model is eligible for a limited important
speaking/action beat when there is a specific baseline fault or a justified
capability need. It is not an automatic full-film upgrade. Use existing evidence
first; if qualification is genuinely necessary, use one already planned useful
shot within the approved budget, not paid A/B takes for every scene. Do not claim
the earlier different-host sample proves same-host compatibility.

Keep the same permitted original identity and supported selected voice reference,
speech intent, lighting and acoustic context across routes. Confirm the returned
take is a clear usable improvement and matches its neighbors during the normal
brief check; a premium label alone is insufficient. Otherwise keep usable baseline
footage, or make the necessary targeted correction in the approved route. Do not
silently recast, dub, raise resolution, buy credits or escalate price. Never reuse
or alter a safety-rejected face/reference on another route to evade that rejection.

## Time and cost discipline

The first representative dialogue piece is an existing planned usable shot. Reuse
its settled setup; let independent nondependent sequences proceed concurrently.
Keep one brief final batch check and targeted inspection for a concrete fault.
No default per-clip ASR, extra contact sheets, paid voice-ranking/review calls,
cosmetic reroll loop or added approval gate. Routine paid review budget remains
USD 0. Use brief permitted local audio inspection or headphones for voice quality;
do not make desktop playback audible without the owner's explicit request. A
waveform, transcript or metadata cannot prove perceived naturalness.

These are evidence-informed production choices, not a verified quality or speed
gain. Record a concrete fault if one occurs and repair only that fault within the
authorized scope/budget. Continue authorized work through export and delivery.
